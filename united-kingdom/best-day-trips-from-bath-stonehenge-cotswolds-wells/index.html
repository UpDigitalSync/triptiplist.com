Best Day Trips from Bath: Stonehenge, Cotswolds & Wells

I spent a week based in Bath, and it’s the perfect launchpad for three very different day trips—each doable without a car if you plan right. Stonehenge is the obvious one, but the Cotswolds and the cathedral city of Wells deserve just as much attention. Here’s what actually worked, what didn’t, and how to avoid wasting half your day in a queue.

Is Stonehenge worth the hype and how do you get there from Bath?

Yes, it’s worth seeing once—but go in with honest expectations. You can’t walk up to the stones anymore. The path loops about 100 meters away, and you’re shuffling along with 200 other people. The magic is in the scale and the silence of the surrounding Salisbury Plain, not the up-close detail.

The easiest way from Bath is the direct bus tour. Several operators run half-day trips from Bath’s bus station (right next to the train station). We took the Scarper Tours minibus at 8:30 AM—15 seats, small group, and they get you there before the big coaches arrive. By 10 AM we had the site mostly to ourselves. Return by 1 PM.

If you want to go independently, take the Great Western Railway from Bath Spa to Salisbury (about 55 minutes), then catch the Stonehenge Tour bus from Salisbury train station. It’s cheaper than a tour but eats more time—figure 4 hours round-trip just in transit.

  • Book ahead: Stonehenge timed-entry slots sell out days in advance in summer. Don’t show up without a reservation.
  • Skip the audio guide: It’s dry. Read the info boards instead.
  • Bring a windbreaker: That plain is exposed. Even in June, we were shivering.
  • Avoid weekends: Midweek mornings are noticeably quieter.

Can you really do the Cotswolds in one day from Bath?

You can, but you have to pick one or two villages, not all of them. The Cotswolds cover 800 square miles—trying to see five villages in a day means more time in the car than out of it.

We hired a car for one day from Enterprise Rent-A-Car on Manvers Street (about £40 for a small automatic). Drove north through Lacock first—the National Trust village where they filmed Harry Potter and Pride & Prejudice. It’s pretty but small; 45 minutes is enough. Then Castle Combe, which is the postcard-perfect one with the bridge and the river. Park in the top lot and walk down.

If you don’t drive, Go Cotswolds runs a small-group day tour from Bath that hits Bibury (Arlington Row), Bourton-on-the-Water (the “Venice of the Cotswolds”—crowded but charming), and Stow-on-the-Wold (antique shops and a quiet market square). The tour stops for lunch in Bourton; we ate at The Rose Tree Restaurant, which does a solid ploughman’s lunch for £12.

  • Lacock: Free entry to the village; the abbey costs £15. Skip the abbey unless you’re a Downton Abbey fan.
  • Castle Combe: Park in the free lot by the school. Don’t drive into the village center—it’s pedestrian-only.
  • Bourton-on-the-Water: Arrive before 10 AM or after 4 PM. Midday is a wall of selfie sticks.
  • Stow-on-the-Wold: Best for a late-afternoon coffee at The Hive Café.

What’s in Wells that makes it worth a day trip from Bath?

Wells is the smallest city in England, but it has three things that make it a better day trip than you’d expect: the Cathedral, the Bishop’s Palace, and Vicar’s Close. The cathedral has a working 14th-century clock (the second-oldest in England) and the scissor arches that look like they’re holding up the ceiling. The Bishop’s Palace has a moat with swans that ring a bell when they want food—yes, they actually learned to do it.

Getting there is straightforward. Take the 376 bus from Bath’s bus station—it runs every 30 minutes and drops you right at the cathedral green in about 75 minutes. Costs £7 return with a contactless card. Or drive in 40 minutes via the A39.

We spent the morning at the cathedral (entry is donation-based, £5 suggested), then walked through Vicar’s Close—a cobbled lane of medieval houses that’s been continuously lived in since 1348. Lunch at The Fountain Inn on the green: decent fish and chips, £14, and you can sit outside watching the cathedral bells ring.

  • Wells Cathedral: Climb the tower tour if you’re fit—90 steps, but you see the medieval scaffolding marks.
  • Bishop’s Palace: Allow 90 minutes. The gardens are quiet even on busy days.
  • Vicar’s Close: Free, always open. Best photo spot in Wells.
  • Market days: Wednesdays and Saturdays have a proper market on the cathedral green. Good for local cheese and pasties.

Should you combine Stonehenge and Glastonbury in one day?

I wouldn’t. They’re only 20 miles apart, but the vibe clash is jarring, and you shortchange both. Stonehenge needs a focused morning; Glastonbury is a half-day of wandering ruins and weird shops. Trying to do both in one day means rushing through the Glastonbury Tor climb (which takes 40 minutes uphill) and skipping the Abbey ruins entirely.

If you have two days, do Stonehenge one day and Glastonbury the next. The Glastonbury Abbey is worth the £9 entry—it’s the legendary burial site of King Arthur, and the grounds are huge and uncrowded. After the abbey, walk up the high street to The Who’d A Thought It pub for a good ale and a plate of local cheddar.

  • Glastonbury Tor: Free. Go early (before 9 AM) to avoid the crowds. The view over the Somerset Levels is the real payoff.
  • Glastonbury Abbey: The apple orchard inside the ruins is a quiet spot for a picnic.
  • Combined tours exist: But they leave at 7 AM and return at 6 PM. That’s 11 hours for two sites you could do in 4.

FAQ

How much time do you need at Stonehenge? Two hours is plenty. The loop walk takes about 45 minutes at a normal pace, and the visitor center has a small exhibition and a reconstructed Neolithic house. Don’t budget more than half a day total including travel from Bath.

Is the Cotswolds better by car or by tour? By car if you want flexibility and can handle narrow lanes with stone walls. By tour if you want to relax and not worry about parking (which is a genuine hassle in Bibury and Bourton-on-the-Water in summer). We did both—car for the first trip, tour for the second—and the tour was less stressful.

Can you visit Wells and the Cotswolds in the same day? Technically yes, but you’d be rushing. Wells is south of Bath, the Cotswolds are north. We tried it once: drove to Wells in the morning, then up to Castle Combe for lunch, then back to Bath by 5 PM. It felt like we saw both places through a car window. Better to give each its own day.

Conclusion

  • Stonehenge is a half-day trip from Bath. Book a small-group minibus tour to beat the crowds.
  • The Cotswolds need a full day. Pick two villages max—Lacock and Castle Combe are my picks.
  • Wells is the underrated gem. Easy bus ride, great cathedral, and the quietest day trip of the three.
  • Don’t combine Stonehenge with Glastonbury in one day unless you enjoy spending more time in transit than at the sites.
  • Book everything in advance—Stonehenge tickets, Cotswolds tours, and even the bus to Wells in summer. Spontaneity is overrated when you’re staring at a “sold out” sign.
