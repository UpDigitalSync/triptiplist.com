Best Day Trips from Lisbon: Sintra, Cascais & More

I spent a week based in Lisbon’s Chiado neighborhood, and every morning I’d look at the map and ask: Where today? The answer was almost always somewhere within an hour by train. Here’s what I actually did, what I’d skip, and what I’d do again.

Why take a day trip from Lisbon instead of staying in the city?

Lisbon is compact, but it’s also loud, hilly, and packed with tourists by 11 AM. A day trip gives you breathing room. You get a different pace—coastal wind instead of tram bells, pine forests instead of pastel buildings. And honestly, the train network from Rossio or Cais do Sodré is so cheap and reliable that you’d be wasting your trip not using it.

I did four day trips in six days. My only regret? Not doing more.

How do you get to Sintra from Lisbon?

Take the train from Rossio Station—it’s right in the center, and the Sintra line departs every 20-30 minutes. Buy a Viva Viagem card (€0.50) and load a round-trip ticket (about €5 total). The ride takes 40 minutes, and you’ll see the suburbs thin out into eucalyptus groves.

Once you arrive at Sintra Station, don’t fall for the tuk-tuk drivers shouting at you. Instead, walk five minutes to the bus stop and take Scotturb line 434 up the hill. It’s a cramped loop, but it drops you at the main sites.

A warning: Sintra gets mobbed by noon. I left Lisbon at 7:30 AM and had the Pena Palace gardens almost to myself. By 10 AM, the line for the palace entrance was 90 minutes long.

What should you actually see in Sintra (and what should you skip)?

  • Pena Palace – The main draw. Yes, it’s Instagram-famous. Yes, it’s worth it. The interior is overstuffed 19th-century kitsch, but the terrace views over the Atlantic are legit. Book tickets online the night before.
  • Castelo dos Mouros – A ruined Moorish castle a 10-minute walk from Pena. If you’re short on time, skip it. The walls are cool, but there’s no shade and the views are similar to Pena’s.
  • Quinta da Regaleira – This was my favorite. It’s a mansion and estate built by an eccentric millionaire, with underground tunnels, fake ruins, and a spiral well you can climb down. Weird and wonderful.
  • Sintra National Palace – In the town center, with those two giant conical chimneys. I skipped it. Friends said it’s fine but not worth the €10 entry if you’ve seen enough azulejo tiles.

For lunch, I walked to Tascantiga on Rua da Ferraria. Small plates of fried choco (cuttlefish) and a glass of vinho verde for under €15. Avoid the tourist traps on the main square—overpriced and bland.

Is Cascais worth the trip from Lisbon?

Yes, but not for the town itself. Cascais is a former fishing village turned resort, and the center is a strip of gelato shops and high-street brands. What makes it worth the 40-minute train from Cais do Sodré is the coastline.

I got off at Cascais Station, walked 10 minutes to the marina, and followed the Boca do Inferno cliff path. That’s the real reason to come. You’ll see sea caves, salt spray, and locals fishing off black rocks. From there, you can walk or rent a bike along the Cascais–Guincho boardwalk—a 5km flat path that runs past the Casa de Santa Maria (a tiny palace-turned-museum) and ends at Praia da Rainha, a sheltered cove beach.

For food, skip the marina restaurants. I ate grilled sardines at Mar do Inferno, a no-frills spot right at the cliff edge. €12 for a full plate with bread and olives.

Can you combine Sintra and Cascais in one day?

Technically yes, but I wouldn’t. The train from Sintra to Cascais takes about an hour (change at Cacém), and you’ll spend more time in transit than seeing either place. If you’re determined, do Sintra early (7 AM–1 PM), then take the 1 PM train to Cascais for a late lunch and a walk to Boca do Inferno. You’ll be back in Lisbon by 7 PM. It’s rushed, but doable.

What are the best day trips from Lisbon besides Sintra and Cascais?

  • Belém – It’s technically Lisbon, but it feels like a day trip. Take tram 15E or the train from Cais do Sodré. See Torre de Belém and Mosteiro dos Jerónimos, then eat a Pastéis de Belém at the original bakery. The line moves fast.
  • Setúbal – 50 minutes by train from Roma-Areeiro Station. A working port town with a massive fish market (Mercado do Livramento) and a ferry to Tróia Peninsula for empty beaches. I ate grilled robalo (sea bass) at Peixola da Beira-Mar for €18.
  • Azenhas do Mar – A tiny whitewashed village on a cliff, 40 minutes by car (or bus from Sintra). There’s a natural swimming pool cut into the rock. Bring a towel and a picnic. No big attractions—just swimming and quiet.
  • Óbidos – A walled medieval town 1 hour north by bus from Campo Grande. It’s touristy (think souvenir shops and cherry liqueur in chocolate cups), but the walls themselves are free to walk and give you a great view of the countryside. Worth a half-day if you’ve already done the coast.

FAQ

Is Sintra too crowded to enjoy? On summer weekends, yes. On weekdays, or if you arrive before 9 AM, it’s manageable. I went on a Tuesday in May and had the Pena Palace terrace to myself for 15 minutes. The crowds arrive in waves from 10 AM to 3 PM. If you can only go on a Saturday, book the first entry slot (9 AM) and leave by lunch.

Do I need a car for these day trips? No. Trains and buses cover all the trips I’ve listed. A car helps for Azenhas do Mar and parts of the Cascais coast, but it’s a hassle for Sintra (parking is expensive and scarce). I used only public transport and never felt limited.

What’s the best time of year for day trips from Lisbon? April–June and September–October. The weather is warm but not scorching, and the crowds are thinner than July–August. I went in early May and had sunny 22°C days. August is hot (30°C+) and every train is packed.

Conclusion

  • Take the 7 AM train to Sintra and head straight to Quinta da Regaleira before the crowds hit Pena Palace.
  • For Cascais, skip the town center and walk the Boca do Inferno cliff path to Praia da Guincho.
  • Combine Sintra and Cascais only if you’re willing to rush—it’s a long travel day.
  • Setúbal and Belém are underrated alternatives when Sintra feels too touristy.
  • Buy a Viva Viagem card at any metro station and keep it loaded—it works on trains, trams, and buses across the region.
