Best Time to Visit Portugal: A Month-by-Month Guide

I’ve spent a lot of time bouncing between Lisbon’s hills, Porto’s riverfront, and the Algarve’s cliffs, and the one thing I’ve learned is that timing is everything. Portugal doesn’t have a single “best” season—it depends on whether you want empty beaches, cheap hotels, or sunny days without melting. This guide breaks down each month so you can pick the window that fits your trip.

When is the best overall time to visit Portugal?

For the sweet spot of good weather, manageable crowds, and reasonable prices, aim for late spring (May to early June) or early autumn (September to October). I’ve hit both windows, and they’re the only times you can comfortably walk Lisbon’s hills without sweating through your shirt or fighting for a table at Time Out Market.

  • May and September offer 20-25°C days, fewer tourists than peak summer, and lower hotel rates.
  • June brings festivals like Porto’s São João, but crowds start building.
  • October still has warm Algarve beaches, though northern Portugal gets rainier.

What is the weather like in Portugal each month?

Portugal’s weather varies more than people expect. The Algarve stays mild in winter, while Porto can get genuinely cold and wet. Here’s what I’ve actually experienced month by month.

January: Cold and rainy in Porto (8-14°C) with gray skies. Lisbon is cooler but drier—I’ve had sunny days exploring Alfama with just a jacket. The Algarve is quiet and around 15°C, but the wind off the Atlantic cuts through you.

February: Still chilly, but almond trees bloom in the Algarve. I once spent a February afternoon at Praia da Marinha with the beach nearly empty.

March: Transition month. Lisbon and Porto get unpredictable—one day sunny, the next drizzly. The Algarve starts warming up, but swimming is still a no-go for me.

April: Spring arrives properly. I’ve strolled through Lisbon’s Jardim Botânico in a light sweater and eaten lunch outside at a tasca in Porto’s Ribeira district.

May: Near-perfect. 22°C in Lisbon, sunny, and the jacaranda trees are in full purple bloom along Avenida da Liberdade. The Algarve hits 25°C—I swam at Praia da Falésia without shivering.

June: Hotter, especially in the Algarve (30°C+). Porto’s São João festival on June 23 is chaotic fun, with crowds banging plastic hammers on each other’s heads.

July: Peak summer. Lisbon is packed and hotel rates double. The Algarve beaches like Benagil Cave are swarmed by tour boats. I avoid July unless I’m heading to the cooler Serra da Estrela mountains.

August: Absolute peak. Prices are highest, beaches are sardine-tight, and Lisbon’s heat can hit 35°C. If you’re in the Algarve, book a spot at Praia do Camilo early or you’ll queue for an hour.

September: My favorite month. The sea is still warm from summer, crowds thin after the first week, and prices drop. I spent a week in Lagos with half the June crowd and perfect 27°C days.

October: Still good in the Algarve (22°C), but Lisbon and Porto get rainier. I’ve had great days exploring Sintra’s Pena Palace without the summer queues.

November: Cold and wet in the north. Porto’s Livraria Lello has shorter lines, but you’ll need a raincoat. The Algarve stays mild enough for hiking the Seven Hanging Valleys trail.

December: Festive but chilly. Lisbon’s Christmas markets at Praça do Comércio are cozy, and Porto’s winter port-wine tastings at Graham’s Lodge are a great rainy-day activity.

When is the cheapest time to visit Portugal?

The cheapest months are January, February, and November (excluding Christmas and New Year’s). I’ve booked a Lisbon Airbnb in January for half the August rate. Flights from the US and UK drop significantly too—I once scored a round-trip to Porto for €180 in February.

  • Low season (Nov-Feb) : Hotels in Lisbon’s Baixa neighborhood drop to €60-80/night. The Algarve’s resorts often close, but budget guesthouses in Lagos stay open.
  • Shoulder season (Mar-Apr, Oct) : Prices are moderate. I stayed at Hotel da Música in Lisbon’s Intendente for €90/night in March.
  • High season (Jun-Aug) : Expect €150+ for basic doubles. Book early or head inland.

When is the best time to visit Lisbon?

I tell friends to visit Lisbon in May or September. The city’s seven hills are brutal in July heat, but in May you can walk from Chiado to Bairro Alto without stopping for water every five minutes. The trams (especially the famous Tram 28) are less packed, and you can actually get a seat at a pastel de nata spot like Manteigaria without queuing.

  • May: Jacaranda trees bloom, outdoor seating at Taberna da Rua das Flores is perfect.
  • September: Still warm enough for a day trip to Cascais or Sintra, but fewer crowds at the Jerónimos Monastery.
  • Avoid August unless you enjoy line-sweating at Belém Tower.

When is the best time to visit Porto?

Porto is best in late spring (May-June) or early autumn (September-October) . The city gets a lot of rain—I’ve had a week straight of drizzle in November—so you want the drier months. June’s São João festival is a blast, but book accommodation early; I once paid €200 for a mediocre room near Avenida dos Aliados.

  • May: Sunny days for port tasting at Sandeman’s cellar in Vila Nova de Gaia.
  • September: Warm enough for a Douro Valley wine tour without the summer crowds.
  • October: Rain risk rises, but the Douro Valley’s autumn colors are stunning.

When is the best time to visit the Algarve?

The Algarve is a seasonal destination. June and September are my picks—the water is warm, the beaches are less packed, and you can actually get a table at a cliffside restaurant like O Camilo in Lagos. July and August are a zoo, with beach umbrellas touching each other at Praia da Rocha.

  • June: Sea temperature hits 20°C, and the crowds haven’t peaked. I kayaked through Benagil Cave with only a short wait.
  • September: Still 25°C air, 22°C sea, and half the tourists. Great for hiking the Costa Vicentina trail.
  • Winter (Dec-Feb) : Many beach bars close, but you’ll have Praia do Vau almost to yourself for long walks.

FAQ

Is Portugal too crowded in August? Yes, especially in the Algarve and Lisbon. I’ve waited 45 minutes for a pastel de nata at Pasteis de Belém in August. If you must go, book everything months in advance and consider less touristy spots like Tavira or the Alentejo coast instead of Albufeira.

Can you swim in the Algarve in winter? Technically yes, but I wouldn’t. The sea hovers around 15-17°C from December to March, and the wind makes it feel colder. I’ve seen locals in wetsuits surfing, but most tourists stick to beach walks and hot chocolate at a café near Praia da Luz.

What’s the best month for hiking in Portugal? March to May and September to October. I’ve done the Rota Vicentina trail in April—wildflowers everywhere, cool mornings, and no midday heat. The Seven Hanging Valleys trail near Marinha Beach is perfect in October, with golden light on the cliffs.

Conclusion

  • Best overall: May or September for weather, crowds, and value.
  • Cheapest: January, February, or November for budget trips.
  • Best beaches: June or September in the Algarve.
  • Best city trip: May for Lisbon, September for Porto.
  • Avoid: August unless you thrive in heat and crowds.
