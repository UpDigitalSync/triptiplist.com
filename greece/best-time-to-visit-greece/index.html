When to Visit Greece: A Month-by-Month Guide for Real Trips

I’ve been to Greece in July, September, and late October, and each trip felt like a completely different country. The difference between a sun-blasted Mykonos in August and a quiet, windy Santorini in November is huge. This guide breaks down exactly when to go based on what you actually want to do — sightseeing in Athens without melting, swimming in the Cyclades without sharing the water with a cruise ship, or finding a hotel under €200 in Oia.

When is the best time to visit Greece for good weather and fewer crowds?

Late spring (mid-May to mid-June) and early autumn (September to early October) are the sweet spots. In May, the sea is still a bit chilly — fine for swimming if you’re tough, but not warm like a bath. The real payoff is empty streets. We walked through Plaka in Athens at 9 a.m. with maybe ten other people. On Santorini, we got a table at a cliff-side café in Fira without a reservation. Temperatures sit around 24-28°C, which is perfect for hiking the caldera trail or climbing the 600 steps from the old port in Fira.

September is even better for swimming. The sea has been baking all summer, so water temps hit 25-26°C. Crowds thin out after the first week, and hotel prices drop by 30-40% compared to August. We stayed at Hotel Thireas in Fira for €120 a night in mid-September — same room was €280 in July.

  • May-June: Best balance of sun, empty sites, and moderate prices
  • September: Warmest sea, fading crowds, good deals on Santorini and Mykonos hotels
  • Avoid: August if you hate queues and €15 gyros

Should I visit Greece in summer (June–August)?

Summer is the obvious choice if your only goal is beach time and nightlife. But it comes with real downsides. In July and August, Athens feels like an oven — the Parthenon at midday is brutal. We lasted 40 minutes at the Acropolis before retreating to the air-conditioned Acropolis Museum for an hour. The city’s metro is packed, and Monastiraki Square is a wall of tourists.

Mykonos in August is a different beast. Paradise Beach is shoulder-to-shoulder sunbeds at €50 each, and the party crowd is loud until 5 a.m. If you want that, fine — but if you want to see the windmills and Little Venice without elbowing through selfie sticks, skip July and August.

  • Pros: Guaranteed sun, all ferries running, nightlife in full swing
  • Cons: Peak prices, 40°C heatwaves in Athens, reservation-only dinners in Oia
  • My advice: Go in June instead of August. Same sun, half the chaos.

What about spring (March–April) or autumn (October–November)?

Spring is underrated for city trips. March is still cold — think 15°C and possible rain — but April warms up nicely. We spent a week in Athens in early April and had the National Archaeological Museum almost to ourselves. The wildflowers on Mount Lycabettus were blooming, and we could sit at a rooftop bar in Plaka without a jacket after sunset.

October is a gamble. The first two weeks are often gorgeous — we had 28°C in Santorini in early October. But by the third week, many tavernas in Oia close for the season, and the wind picks up. Mykonos turns into a ghost town by late October; some hotels shut down entirely. November is grey and wet. I wouldn't recommend it unless you’re strictly visiting Athens for museums and don’t mind rain.

  • April: Best for Athens sightseeing, cheap flights, blooming landscapes
  • Early October: Last window for warm island days, but check ferry schedules
  • November: Only for budget travelers who accept cold and drizzle

When is the cheapest time to visit Greece?

November through February is the low season, and prices reflect that. Flights from the US or UK can be half the cost of summer. We booked a room at Athens Gate Hotel (right next to the Temple of Olympian Zeus) for €65 a night in January. The downside: many island ferries run reduced schedules, and Santorini’s cable car from the old port often has maintenance closures. You’ll be walking those 600 steps in the rain.

Winter is fine for Athens — the Ancient Agora and Kerameikos are nearly empty. But the islands are genuinely dead. On Mykonos, we found exactly three restaurants open in February. One of them served us frozen souvlaki. I’d only do a winter island trip if you want solitude and don’t mind cold, windy walks.

  • Cheapest months: January, February, November
  • Trade-off: Limited ferry routes, closed hotels, cold sea
  • Best winter bet: Stick to Athens and add a day trip to Delphi or Cape Sounion

How do the islands differ by season?

Santorini and Mykonos have opposite seasonal rhythms. Santorini is most beautiful in shoulder seasons because the volcanic caldera looks dramatic even under clouds. We went in late October and watched a thunderstorm roll over Fira — it was incredible. The wine tours at Santo Wines were still running, and we had the tasting room to ourselves.

Mykonos lives for summer. The island’s charm is its beaches and nightlife, both of which shut down by mid-October. If you visit Mykonos in May, expect half-empty beach clubs and windy afternoons. Paradise Beach in May is quiet enough to hear the waves — which some people love, but if you want the party, don't come before June.

  • Santorini: Best in May, September, early October
  • Mykonos: Best in June or September (not August)
  • Both islands: Avoid November–March unless you’re fine with closed shops and cold wind

FAQ

What is the rainiest month in Greece? December and January are the wettest, especially in Athens and the northern mainland. The Cyclades (Santorini, Mykonos) are drier year-round, but winter storms can still cancel ferries for a day or two. If you’re island-hopping in winter, build in a buffer day.

Can I swim in the sea in May or October? Yes, but it depends on your tolerance. The sea in May is around 19-20°C — refreshing, not warm. By October, it’s still 22-23°C from summer heat. We swam off Red Beach in Santorini in early October and it was fine for 20 minutes. If you need bathtub-warm water, stick to July–September.

Is Greece too crowded in July? Yes, especially the islands. Santorini’s Oia gets gridlocked with cruise ship day-trippers from 11 a.m. to 4 p.m. Mykonos’s main town is a slow-moving human river. Athens is better — the crowds are concentrated at the Acropolis and Plaka, but the rest of the city is manageable. If July is your only option, book everything in advance and plan early morning sightseeing.

Conclusion

  • For city culture and low prices: Visit Athens in April, October, or even January
  • For swimming and empty beaches: Go in September — warm sea, quiet islands, fair prices
  • For Santorini sunsets without the crush: May or early October beat July by a mile
  • For Mykonos nightlife: June or September deliver the party without August insanity
  • For budget travelers: November through February works only if you stay on the mainland and love solitude
