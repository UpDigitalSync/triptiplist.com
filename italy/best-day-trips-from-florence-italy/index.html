Best Day Trips from Florence: Siena, Pisa, Lucca & More

I’ve spent years making the same mistake on trips to Florence: packing too much into one day. After burning out on a single “see everything in Tuscany” tour, I learned that the best day trips from Florence work best when you pick one town and give it a real afternoon. Here’s what actually worked on my last five trips—transport times, honest opinions on what’s worth it, and a few places I’d skip next time.

Is Siena worth the trip from Florence?

Yes—but only if you commit to a full day. The bus from Florence’s Busitalia station (right next to Santa Maria Novella) takes about 75 minutes and drops you at Piazza Gramsci. Avoid the train: it’s slower and drops you at a station that’s a 20-minute uphill walk from the historic center.

Start at Piazza del Campo, the shell-shaped main square. Climb the Torre del Mangia for the view—it’s 400 steps, but you see the entire Val d’Orcia on a clear day. Then walk to Duomo di Siena; the marble floor mosaics are worth the €8 entry. For lunch, skip the tourist spots on the square. Walk two blocks to Osteria Le Logge on Via dei Rossi—their pici cacio e pepe is the best I’ve had in Tuscany.

  • Transport: Bus from Florence (75 min, €8-12 one way)
  • Must see: Piazza del Campo, Torre del Mangia, Duomo floor mosaics
  • Lunch pick: Osteria Le Logge (Via dei Rossi)
  • Time needed: 6-7 hours minimum

Can you do San Gimignano as a day trip?

You can, but I’d pair it with a stop in another town unless you have a car. The train from Florence to Poggibonsi takes about an hour, then you catch a 20-minute bus up the hill. If you drive, it’s 50 minutes from Florence. The town itself is small—you can walk the main street in 20 minutes. The famous Torri di San Gimignano (the medieval towers) look great from the road, but climbing one is steep and narrow; I’d save the energy for Siena’s tower instead.

What I do like: Gelateria Dondoli on Piazza della Cisterna. The owner has won world gelato championships, and the “Crema di Santa Fina” with saffron and pine nuts is genuinely unique. Also, the Collegiata di Santa Maria Assunta has frescoes that rival anything in Florence, and it’s never crowded.

  • Transport: Train to Poggibonsi + bus (90 min total) or car (50 min)
  • Best gelato: Gelateria Dondoli
  • Underrated spot: Collegiata frescoes
  • Time needed: 3-4 hours—enough to feel rushed but satisfied

What’s the best way to see Pisa without wasting time?

Everyone goes to Pisa for the Leaning Tower. I’ll be blunt: the Piazza dei Miracoli is impressive for about 15 minutes. The crowds are relentless, and the “holding up the tower” photos get old fast. But the rest of Pisa is a real city with good food and quiet streets. Take the Trenitalia Regionale from Florence to Pisa Centrale (1 hour, €9). Walk 20 minutes through the Borgo Stretto shopping street to get to the tower—that walk is more interesting than the tower itself.

If you want to climb the tower, book tickets weeks ahead on the official Opera della Primaziale Pisana site. I skipped the climb and instead visited the Camposanto Monumentale (the cemetery) next to the cathedral. The frescoes there, especially “The Triumph of Death,” are haunting and uncrowded.

  • Train: Regionale from Florence (1 hr, €9 one way)
  • Tower tickets: Book at opapisa.it at least 2 weeks ahead
  • Better than the tower: Camposanto Monumentale
  • Lunch spot: Osteria del Porton Rosso (Via del Castelletto, 20 min from Piazza dei Miracoli)

Why should I visit Lucca instead of the other towns?

Lucca is the most relaxing day trip from Florence, and that’s its superpower. The train from Florence to Lucca Centrale takes 80 minutes on the Regionale (€8). The town is flat, encircled by intact Renaissance walls that are now a park. Rent a bike from Poli Bici right outside the station (€5 per hour) and ride the 4.2 km loop on top of the walls. It takes 20 minutes at a slow pace, and you see the whole city from above.

Inside the walls, head to Piazza dell’Anfiteatro, an oval square built on the ruins of a Roman amphitheater. The Torre Guinigi has oak trees growing on top—climb it for the best view of the wall’s green belt. For lunch, Da Gherardo on Via del Gallo does a €15 pasta-and-glass-of-wine lunch that’s better than any tourist menu I’ve had in Florence.

  • Train: Regionale from Florence (80 min, €8 one way)
  • Bike rental: Poli Bici (€5/hour, near station)
  • Best view: Torre Guinigi
  • Lunch: Da Gherardo (Via del Gallo)

Can you combine two towns in one day?

I’ve tried it. It’s exhausting. The only combo that works smoothly is Lucca + Pisa because they’re on the same train line west of Florence. Take the 8:00 AM Regionale from Florence to Lucca (80 min), spend 4 hours there, then take a 25-minute train to Pisa (€3), spend 2-3 hours at the tower, and train back to Florence. You’ll be tired by 7 PM, but you’ll have seen two complete towns.

Do not try Siena + San Gimignano in one day without a rental car. Buses between them are infrequent, and you’ll spend more time waiting than sightseeing.

Should you rent a car for these day trips?

Only if you’re doing three or more day trips. Car rental from Florence Airport or Santa Maria Novella starts around €50/day with manual transmission (automatic is €70+). The ZTL (limited traffic zones) in Siena, San Gimignano, and Lucca are aggressive—drive into one without a permit and you’ll get a €100 fine mailed to your home months later. Park at the Parcheggio Santa Caterina outside Siena’s walls (€2/hour) or at the Campo di Marte lot in Lucca (€1.50/hour). Trains are cheaper and less stressful for solo travelers or couples.

FAQ

What is the cheapest way to do day trips from Florence? Buy a Trenitalia Regionale pass or individual tickets at the station kiosk. A day of two round-trip tickets (Florence to Lucca and Lucca to Pisa) costs about €25 total. Avoid the high-speed Frecciarossa trains for these short trips—they’re faster but cost triple and don’t stop at the smaller stations you need.

Which day trip is best for food lovers? Siena, hands down. The Mercato di Piazza del Campo on Wednesday mornings has local cheese, cured meats, and olive oil samples. Osteria Le Logge and Trattoria La Torre both serve traditional Tuscan dishes without the Florence markup.

How early should I leave Florence for a day trip? Leave by 7:30 AM for any destination. The 8:00 AM trains are less crowded, and you’ll arrive before the tour buses (which start rolling in around 10:30 AM). Return by the 6:00 PM train to avoid the evening rush and have dinner back in Florence.

Conclusion

  • Best single trip: Siena—give it a full day for the duomo and piazza
  • Best quick trip: Lucca—bike the walls, eat at Da Gherardo, leave by 3 PM
  • Best combo: Lucca + Pisa on the same train line
  • Skip San Gimignano unless you have a car or a strong gelato craving
  • Don’t rent a car unless you’re doing three trips—trains are easier and cheaper
