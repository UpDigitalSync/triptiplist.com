Best Time to Visit Spain: A Month-by-Month Guide

I’ve spent a lot of time bouncing between the three big Spanish cities—Barcelona, Madrid, and Seville—and I’ve made every timing mistake possible. I’ve roasted on a Seville rooftop in August, shivered through a Madrid February, and fought tourist herds in Barcelona’s Gothic Quarter in July. This guide is the cheat sheet I wish I’d had: practical, city-specific advice on when to go, what you’ll pay, and what you’ll actually experience.

When is the best weather in Spain?

Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to November) are your sweet spots. The weather is comfortable across all three cities without being extreme.

  • Barcelona: Best in April–May and September–October. The sea is swimmable by late May, and the Ramblas are busy but not suffocating.
  • Madrid: May and October are ideal. The city empties out for the August holiday, so spring and fall feel more local.
  • Seville: March–April and October–November. Seville’s April Fair (Feria de Abril) is a cultural highlight, but book accommodation months ahead. Avoid July and August unless you enjoy 40°C (104°F) shade.

I once visited Seville in early June and still struggled with the heat. Stick to spring or fall if you want to actually enjoy walking through Plaza de España without melting.

What is the cheapest time to visit Spain?

Winter (November through February, excluding Christmas and New Year’s) is your budget window. Flights and hotels drop significantly.

  • Barcelona: January and February are the cheapest months. We stayed at Hotel Barcelona 1882 near the Sagrada Familia for half the July rate. The Boqueria Market is less crowded, and you can grab a seat at El Quim de la Boqueria without waiting.
  • Madrid: November and January offer the best deals. The Museo del Prado is quieter, and tapas bars in La Latina neighborhood feel local again.
  • Seville: January is dead cheap, but some smaller restaurants close for the month. February is a better bet—still low season, but more places open.

One catch: winter days are short (sunset around 6 PM), so plan your sightseeing for the morning. We learned that the hard way trying to cram the Alcázar into a late-afternoon slot in December.

When are the biggest crowds in Spain?

Peak season runs June through August, plus Semana Santa (the week before Easter) and Christmas. If you hate queues, avoid these.

  • Barcelona: August is a nightmare. The Sagrada Familia line stretches around the block by 9 AM. Park Güell requires a timed ticket booked weeks in advance. We skipped it entirely one August and went to Montjuïc Castle instead—much quieter.
  • Madrid: July and August are hot and crowded, but the city actually empties out as locals flee to the coast. The Royal Palace still draws big lines, though.
  • Seville: Semana Santa (March/April) is packed beyond belief. The processions are incredible, but you’ll be standing for hours. Feria de Abril is also mobbed. If you want to see the Metropol Parasol without a photo-bombing crowd, go in late September.

My rule of thumb: if you see a photo of La Sagrada Familia with no people in it, that photo was taken at 7 AM in February. Plan accordingly.

What are the best festivals and events in Spain?

If you want to plan your trip around a celebration, here’s what actually delivers:

  • La Mercè (Barcelona, late September): Free concerts, castells (human towers), and fireworks. The city feels alive without the August crush. We caught a free show at Plaça de Catalunya and it was a highlight.
  • San Isidro (Madrid, mid-May): Madrid’s patron saint festival with bullfights (controversial, but part of the tradition), concerts, and fairgrounds in El Retiro Park. The park itself is gorgeous in May.
  • Feria de Abril (Seville, two weeks after Easter): Flamenco, horse parades, and endless sherry. Stay in Santa Cruz neighborhood for walking access, but book a room at Hotel Doña María months ahead—it overlooks the cathedral and fills up fast.

I’ll be honest: I found La Tomatina (Buñol, near Valencia) overrated—it’s a tomato-throwing mosh pit. Skip it unless you’re 22 and love being sticky.

How does the weather compare by city month to month?

Here’s a quick breakdown so you can decide without digging through ten tabs:

  • Barcelona: Mild winters (12–15°C), warm springs (18–22°C), hot summers (28–30°C), pleasant autumns (20–24°C). The Barceloneta beach is usable from June to September, but the water is cold until July.
  • Madrid: Cold winters (5–10°C, sometimes snow), pleasant springs (15–20°C), scorching summers (35–40°C), cool autumns (15–20°C). The Plaza Mayor is lovely in spring but an oven in July.
  • Seville: Hot year-round. Winters are mild (10–15°C), springs are warm (20–25°C), summers are brutal (35–45°C), autumns are pleasant (20–25°C). I’d only visit Seville in summer if you have a pool. The Hotel Alfonso XIII has a beautiful one, but it’s pricey.

Pro tip: In Madrid and Seville, the siesta is real for a reason. We learned to sightsee from 9 AM–1 PM, then eat lunch, nap, and resume at 5 PM. It saved our sanity in July.

What about holidays and school breaks?

Avoid the last week of December (Christmas), the first week of January (New Year’s), and the week of Semana Santa. These spike prices and crowds across all three cities.

  • Barcelona: Sant Jordi (April 23) is a lovely local holiday—books and roses everywhere—but it’s not a major travel disruption.
  • Madrid: Dos de Mayo (May 2) is a regional holiday. The city feels festive, but some museums close.
  • Seville: Semana Santa is the biggest event of the year. The city doubles in population. If you don’t have accommodation booked by January, you’re paying triple.

I once landed in Madrid on December 30 and couldn’t find a table for dinner without a reservation. Casa Lucio (famous for huevos rotos) was booked solid. Plan your meals ahead during holiday weeks.

FAQ

What is the absolute worst month to visit Spain? August, without question. Barcelona and Seville are overcrowded and uncomfortably hot. Madrid is slightly better because many locals leave, but you’ll still face 38°C afternoons. If you can only go in summer, pick late September or early June instead.

Can I visit Barcelona, Madrid, and Seville in one trip? Yes, but give yourself at least 10 days. Take the AVE high-speed train between cities—it’s faster than flying. Madrid to Seville is 2.5 hours, and Barcelona to Madrid is 2.5 hours. We did it in 12 days and felt rushed. 14 days is more comfortable.

Is Spain cheaper in winter? Yes, but only if you skip Christmas and New Year’s. January is the cheapest month for flights and hotels across all three cities. Just pack a jacket for Madrid (it gets cold) and expect shorter daylight hours. The Alcázar of Seville is still stunning in winter, and the gardens are lush from rain.
