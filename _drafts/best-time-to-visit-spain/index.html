Best Time to Visit Spain: A Month-by-Month Guide for 2025

I’ve been to Spain in every season except maybe deep January, and I’ve made plenty of mistakes—like trying to walk through the Alcázar of Seville at 2 PM in August. This guide is what I wish I’d had before booking. We’ll cover the real trade-offs for Barcelona, Madrid, and Seville: when the heat is bearable, when the crowds thin out, and when your budget stretches furthest. No fluff, just what works.

When is the best overall time to visit Spain?

For most people, the sweet spot is April to June and September to early October. The weather is warm but not punishing, the days are long, and the tourist masses haven’t fully arrived yet. I’ve done both spring and fall trips, and I slightly prefer fall—the light in Madrid around late September is golden, and the crowds at the Alhambra in Granada thin out just enough to make the queue tolerable.

  • Spring (April–June): Wildflowers in Andalusia, comfortable walking weather. Barcelona’s Barceloneta beach is pleasant but not packed.
  • Fall (September–October): Harvest season in La Rioja, lower hotel rates after mid-September. We stayed at Hotel Casa Fuster in Barcelona for 30% less than August rates.
  • Winter (November–February): Cheapest flights, but short days. Seville’s Real Alcázar gardens are still green, and you’ll have Plaza de España almost to yourself on a weekday.

What’s the best time to visit Barcelona?

I’ve landed in Barcelona in July and regretted it immediately. The humidity off the Mediterranean turns the Rambla del Raval into a sweatbox. The best time is May or September. In May, the Parc de la Ciutadella is full of locals reading in the sun, and the queues for Sagrada Família are manageable if you book the 9 AM slot. September still has beach weather, but the Gothic Quarter feels less like a theme park.

  • Avoid: August—locals flee the city, and the Barceloneta beach is shoulder-to-shoulder. Also, many small restaurants close for two weeks.
  • Our pick: We booked a walking tour of El Born neighborhood in early October and had the Basilica of Santa Maria del Mar nearly to ourselves.
  • Budget tip: Hotel rates in L’Eixample drop by 40% in November, but pack a jacket for the evening wind off the port.

When should you visit Madrid?

Madrid is a high-altitude city, so it gets genuinely cold in winter (I wore a wool coat in January) and scorching in July. The best window is late April through early June. I spent a long weekend in Madrid in mid-May, and the Mercado de San Miguel was lively without being jammed. The Retiro Park boat rentals were open, and we ate lunch at Casa Lucio without a reservation.

  • Spring: The Royal Palace gardens bloom. Plaza Mayor is perfect for a mid-afternoon coffee.
  • Fall: September and October are mild. We stayed at The Principal Madrid on Gran Vía and could walk to Museo del Prado without sweating.
  • Winter: Cheaper flights, but the Plaza de Cibeles fountain is often dry. The Rastro flea market on Sundays is less crowded.

What about Seville—when is it not too hot?

Seville is the hottest city in mainland Spain, and it’s not subtle. I made the mistake of visiting in mid-July, and the Plaza de España felt like a pizza oven by 11 AM. The best time is March to May or October to November. In March, the Alcázar gardens are in full bloom with orange trees, and the Metropol Parasol (the giant wooden mushroom structure) has short lines.

  • Spring: Semana Santa (Holy Week) is spectacular but insane—book hotels a year ahead. We loved the Hotel Alfonso XIII but it was €450 a night.
  • Fall: October is perfect. We ate tapas at El Rinconcillo (the oldest bar in Seville, open since 1670) and sat outside comfortably.
  • Avoid: July and August. Even locals disappear to the coast. The Triana neighborhood feels like a ghost town at midday.

Should you visit Spain in summer anyway?

If you only have summer vacation, don’t skip Spain—just adjust your schedule. The trick is to embrace the siesta rhythm. In Barcelona, we swam at Bogatell Beach (less crowded than Barceloneta) at 7 PM, then ate dinner at Can Solé near the Barceloneta metro after 9 PM. In Madrid, we did the Prado Museum at the free entry hour (6–8 PM) and ate late at Casa Toni near Sol.

  • Beach towns: Swap inland cities for Costa Brava or San Sebastián in July. The water cools you down.
  • Air conditioning: Most hotels in L’Eixample and Salamanca neighborhoods have solid AC. We stayed at Hotel 1898 on Las Ramblas and the room was a fridge.
  • Night culture: Summer is when Madrid’s rooftop bars (like Círculo de Bellas Artes) come alive. The Mercado de la Cebada in La Latina has outdoor tables until 1 AM.

What’s the cheapest time to visit Spain?

November through February (excluding Christmas and New Year’s week) is the cheapest window. Flights from the US and UK drop by half, and hotels in Madrid’s Barrio de las Letras or Barcelona’s El Raval can be under €80 a night. I visited Seville in early December and paid €60 for a room at Hotel Las Casas de la Judería.

  • Downsides: Short days (sunset at 6 PM). Rain is common in Madrid and Barcelona.
  • Upsides: The Prado and Reina Sofía museums have no queues. Seville’s Cathedral is uncrowded.
  • Festivals: Three Kings’ Day (January 6) is huge in Seville—parades and family events, but book ahead.

FAQ

Is it better to visit Spain in spring or fall? Spring (April–June) has more festivals like Feria de Abril in Seville, but fall (September–October) has fewer crowds and lower prices. I prefer fall because the weather is still warm enough for outdoor dining in Madrid’s La Latina without the Easter-week chaos.

Can I visit Barcelona, Madrid, and Seville in one trip? Yes, but don’t try it in July or August—the heat between cities will drain you. I did a 10-day loop in May: fly into Barcelona, take the AVE high-speed train to Madrid (2.5 hours), then another AVE to Seville (2.5 hours). The AVE is comfortable and punctual.

What month has the least tourists? January and February have the fewest tourists, but many small museums and restaurants in Seville’s Santa Cruz neighborhood close for winter holidays. November is a good middle ground—still quiet, but most places are open.

Conclusion

  • For best weather and manageable crowds: April–June or September–October. May in Barcelona, late September in Madrid.
  • For budget travelers: November–February. Skip Christmas week. Book Hotel Las Casas de la Judería in Seville for winter deals.
  • For festival lovers: Spring (Semana Santa in Seville) or summer (San Fermín in Pamplona, but be ready for crowds).
  • For beach fans: June or September. Avoid August on the Costa del Sol.
  • Avoid July and August in Seville and Madrid unless you’re okay with 40°C heat and empty streets during midday.
