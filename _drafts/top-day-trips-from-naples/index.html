Top Day Trips from Naples for Smart Travelers

The first time I based myself in Naples for a week, I thought I’d be bored by day three. Instead, I barely scratched the surface. Naples is the perfect launchpad for four very different day trips — each reachable in under an hour by train or ferry. Here’s what actually worked, what didn’t, and where I’d skip the hype.

Is Pompeii worth the early alarm?

Yes, but only if you arrive before 9:00 AM. I made the mistake of rolling in at 11:00 once, and spent more time shuffling behind tour groups than actually seeing the ruins. The Circumvesuviana train from Napoli Garibaldi drops you right at Pompeii Scavi station in about 35 minutes. Buy tickets online in advance — the queue at the gate can eat 40 minutes in peak season.

  • Pompeii Archaeological Park — focus on the Forum, the Villa of the Mysteries, and the Lupanar brothel. Skip the plaster casts area if you’re short on time; it’s morbid and crowded.
  • Lunch at Caupona — just outside the main entrance. Simple, affordable panini and decent espresso. Avoid the overpriced tourist spots directly on Via Plinio.
  • Herculaneum — if you have a second day, take the same train one stop further. Smaller, better preserved, and way fewer people. I actually preferred it.

How do you get to Capri without wasting half the day?

The ferry from Molo Beverello in Naples runs hourly from 6:30 AM to around 6:00 PM. I booked a Caremar hydrofoil for €22 each way — it took 50 minutes. The mistake most people make is trying to see the Blue Grotto. I waited 90 minutes in a rowboat line and got 3 minutes inside. Skip it.

  • Take the chairlift to Monte Solaro from Anacapri. The view over the whole gulf is better than anything you’ll see from the water.
  • Lunch at Da Gelsomina on Via Migliara. Their homemade ravioli caprese is the best meal I had on the island. Book a table.
  • Walk the Pizzolungo path from the Faraglioni rocks back to Marina Piccola. It’s a 45-minute walk with no crowds and real coastal views.
  • Don’t buy limoncello in the main square of Capri town. It’s triple the price of the same bottles in Anacapri.

Is Sorrento just a tourist trap or worth the trip?

Sorrento is the most balanced day trip from Naples — easy logistics, good food, and actual local character if you walk ten minutes off the main drag. The Campania Express (the pricier, air-conditioned version of the Circumvesuviana) gets you there in about an hour from Napoli Garibaldi. Pay the €13 instead of the €4 local train; you’ll thank me in July.

  • Hotel Antiche Mura — I didn’t stay here, but their rooftop bar has the best view of Mount Vesuvius at sunset. Grab a Negroni even if you’re not a guest.
  • Lunch at Osteria del Buonconvento on Vico Fuoro. Their spaghetti alle vongole is simple and perfect. No English menu, no photos on the wall — that’s how you know it’s good.
  • Walk the Villa Comunale park for a free panoramic terrace. Then take the stairs down to Marina Grande for a swim.
  • Skip the limoncello factory tours in town. They’re all the same scripted pitch. Buy a bottle at a fruttivendolo instead.

Can you really do the Amalfi Coast as a day trip?

You can, but you need to be smart about it. Driving is a nightmare — I watched a rental car side-swipe a scooter on the Amalfi Drive in real time. Instead, take the SITA bus from Sorrento (which you reach via train from Naples) or book a ferry directly from Naples to Amalfi town. The ferry takes 75 minutes and costs €15 each way. It’s worth the extra money.

  • Amalfi — focus on the Duomo di Sant’Andrea and the Chiostro del Paradiso. The paper museum is skippable unless you’re really into stationery.
  • Ravello — take the bus up from Amalfi (30 minutes, €2.50). Villa Rufolo’s gardens are genuinely stunning, and the crowds thin out after 4:00 PM.
  • Positano — I’d skip it as a day trip. It’s beautiful to look at from a boat, but the beach is tiny, the stairs are brutal, and the prices are double Amalfi’s.
  • Lunch at Da Gemma in Amalfi — their scialatielli ai frutti di mare is the best pasta I ate on the coast. Reserve or go at 2:30 PM when the rush passes.

Should you buy a combination ticket or go a la carte?

For Pompeii and Herculaneum, the Campania ArteCard saves money if you’re doing two or more sites. It covers entry to Pompeii, Herculaneum, the Naples National Archaeological Museum, and unlimited local transport for three days (€32). For Capri and the Amalfi Coast, pay as you go — the ferry tickets and single-entry fees don’t bundle well.

  • Napoli Garibaldi station — buy the ArteCard at the official kiosk near track 1. The machines don’t accept cash.
  • Trenitalia — for longer trips like Rome or Caserta, book regional trains on the app. The high-speed Frecciarossa is faster but doesn’t stop at Pompeii or Sorrento.

FAQ

What’s the best time of year for day trips from Naples? April to June and September to October. July and August are brutally hot, and ferries get cancelled in rough weather. I went in late May and had clear skies with manageable crowds.

Can I use a rental car for these day trips? I wouldn’t. Parking in Pompeii is a mess, Capri has no cars, and the Amalfi Drive is a stress bomb. The trains and ferries are cheaper and faster. If you must drive, use a garage in Sorrento and take the bus from there.

Which day trip should I skip if I only have time for three? Skip Capri if you hate lines and overpriced food. Do Pompeii, Sorrento, and Amalfi instead. You’ll see more, spend less, and actually relax.

Conclusion

  • Pompeii is best before 9 AM — arrive by Circumvesuviana from Napoli Garibaldi.
  • Capri works if you skip the Blue Grotto and head to Anacapri for the chairlift.
  • Sorrento is the easiest all-rounder — good food, easy train, and a real town feel.
  • Amalfi Coast is doable by ferry from Naples, but skip Positano and focus on Amalfi town plus Ravello.
  • Buy the Campania ArteCard only if you’re combining Pompeii, Herculaneum, and the Archaeological Museum.
