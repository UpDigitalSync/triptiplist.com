Top Day Trips from Naples: Pompeii, Capri & Amalfi

I landed in Naples with three days and a loose plan to see more than just the city’s chaotic streets and killer pizza. Every local I talked to said the same thing: “You have to get out of Naples for at least one day.” So I did. Here’s what actually worked, what didn’t, and how to stack your days without wasting half of them in transit.

Is Pompeii worth a full day, or can you do it in a half-day trip?

Pompeii is huge. I made the mistake of thinking I could breeze through in three hours. I couldn’t. If you’re serious about seeing the Forum, the Villa of the Mysteries, and the plaster casts in the Garden of the Fugitives, plan for five to six hours minimum. That said, a half-day works if you focus on the western zone—the brothel, the amphitheater, and the main square.

  • Train: Take the Circumvesuviana from Napoli Centrale to Pompei Scavi—it’s €3.10 each way and runs every 30 minutes.
  • Ticket: Buy online from the official Pompeii Sites website to skip the queue at the Porta Marina entrance.
  • Tip: Go early (8:30 AM opening) or late (after 2 PM) to avoid the cruise-ship crowds.
  • Lunch: Skip the overpriced cafeteria inside the ruins. Walk five minutes to Pizzeria da Franco on Via Roma for a €5 margherita that’s better than most Naples spots.

How do you actually get to Capri without losing your mind?

Capri is the most scenic day trip from Naples, but it’s also the most logistically annoying. You’ve got two options: the hydrofoil from Molo Beverello (50 minutes, €22 one way) or the slower ferry from Calata Porta di Massa (80 minutes, €17). I took the hydrofoil—it’s bumpy but fast. Book tickets on TraghettiOnline the night before; don’t rely on walk-up availability in summer.

  • Arrive early: The 8:15 AM hydrofoil gets you there before the herd from Sorrento.
  • Skip the Blue Grotto: It’s a 45-minute wait for a 5-minute boat ride. Instead, hire a private boat from Capri Whales for €50 per person—they take you around the Faraglioni rocks and into the grotto without the line.
  • Walk to Villa Jovis: It’s a steep 40-minute hike from the Piazzetta, but you get the whole island to yourself up there.
  • Lunch: Da Paolino on Via Palazzo a Mare—eat under the lemon trees. Their spaghetti al limone is the real deal, not tourist gimmick.

Sorrento: Is it just a tourist trap, or worth the stop?

Sorrento gets a bad rap for being the Amalfi Coast’s gateway town—full of souvenir shops and package-tour hotels. And yeah, the main street (Corso Italia) is a gauntlet of limoncello stalls. But the old town behind Piazza Tasso has genuine charm. I spent an afternoon wandering the quiet lanes near Villa Comunale—the view of Mount Vesuvius from the public gardens is better than any paid viewpoint.

  • How to get there: Circumvesuviana from Naples to Sorrento—70 minutes, €4.30. Sit on the left side for coastal views after Castellammare.
  • What to do: Visit the Sedil Dominova (a 15th-century noble meeting hall) and the tiny Museo Correale for a quiet hour of art and lemon-scented gardens.
  • Eat: Osteria San Francesco on Via San Francesco—order the gnocchi alla sorrentina. It’s not on the main drag, so it’s mostly locals.
  • Skip: The Marina Grande beach. It’s rocky, packed, and the restaurants there charge double for the same fried seafood you can get inland.

Is the Amalfi Coast doable as a day trip from Naples?

Short answer: yes, but only if you’re okay with being on a bus or ferry for half the day. I tried the bus from Sorrento to Amalfi—the famous Route 10 along the SS163—and it’s exactly as terrifying as everyone says. Narrow curves, sheer drops, and drivers who treat the road like a racetrack. The ferry from Naples to Amalfi (via Travelmar) is more relaxing: 90 minutes, €15, with views of Positano you can’t get from the road.

  • Ferry schedule: Only runs April–October. Check Travelmar’s website for the latest times.
  • Focus on Amalfi town: Skip Positano unless you want to spend two hours in traffic. Amalfi’s cathedral (Duomo di Sant’Andrea) and the Chiostro del Paradiso are worth the trip alone.
  • Lunch: Da Gemma on Via Fra Gerardo Sasso—their scialatielli ai frutti di mare is the best pasta I had on the coast. Reservations mandatory in summer.
  • Return timing: The last ferry back to Naples leaves Amalfi around 6 PM. If you miss it, you’re looking at a 2-hour bus ride to Sorrento, then the train.

What about Herculaneum instead of Pompeii?

Herculaneum is smaller, better preserved, and way less crowded than Pompeii. If you’ve only got a half-day, this is the smarter choice. The ruins are more intact because the volcanic mud baked them into a hard shell—you can still see wooden furniture, carbonized food, and mosaics with original colors. I spent three hours here and felt like I saw everything.

  • Train: Same Circumvesuviana line, get off at Ercolano Scavi. €2.80 each way.
  • Ticket: €13, or €20 for the combined ticket with Pompeii (valid 3 days).
  • Don’t miss: The House of the Deer (Casa dei Cervi) for its garden frescoes, and the Skeleton House (Casa dello Scheletro) for the eerie human remains.
  • Lunch: Antica Osteria Nonna Rosa on Via Roma—their pasta e patate con provola is a local classic.

FAQ

Can you combine Pompeii and Sorrento in one day? Yes, but it’s tight. Take the 7:30 AM Circumvesuviana from Naples to Pompei Scavi, spend three hours at the ruins (focus on the Forum and the amphitheater), then catch the 12:15 PM train to Sorrento. You’ll arrive by 1:15 PM, have lunch, wander the old town, and catch the 6 PM train back. You won’t see everything, but it’s doable.

What’s the best time of year for day trips from Naples? April–May and September–October. June through August is a zoo—Capri’s Piazzetta is shoulder-to-shoulder by 11 AM, and the Amalfi bus queues stretch for 45 minutes. I went in mid-May and had mild weather without the peak-season crowds.

Do I need to book tickets in advance for Pompeii or Capri? For Pompeii, yes—especially if you want the guided tour. The official site has timed entry slots. For Capri, book the hydrofoil ticket online the night before; same-day tickets often sell out by 9 AM in summer.

Conclusion

  • Pompeii needs a half-day minimum; go early and skip the on-site lunch.
  • Capri is beautiful but logistically annoying—take the first hydrofoil and skip the Blue Grotto line.
  • Sorrento is worth a few hours for the views and the gnocchi, but don’t plan a full day there.
  • Amalfi Coast is best done by ferry from Naples—buses are stressful and slow.
  • Herculaneum is the underrated pick if you’re short on time or crowd-averse.
