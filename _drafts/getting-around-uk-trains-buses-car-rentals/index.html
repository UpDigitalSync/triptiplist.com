Getting Around the UK: Trains, Buses & Car Rentals

I’ve spent the last few years bouncing between London, Edinburgh, and Manchester for work and trips, and the UK’s transport system is a mixed bag—brilliant when it works, frustrating when it doesn’t. This guide cuts through the noise: which train pass actually saves money, when to rent a car instead of taking the bus, and the one thing nobody tells you about driving in Edinburgh. No fluff, just what I’ve learned the hard way.

Should I take the train or the bus between cities?

It depends on your budget and patience. Trains are faster but pricey; buses are cheap but slow. For London to Edinburgh, I always take the LNER train from King’s Cross—it’s 4.5 hours and you can book an advance single for around £40 if you plan two weeks ahead. The Avanti West Coast line from London to Manchester takes just over two hours, and I’ve scored tickets for £25 by booking at 10 AM on a Tuesday (their release schedule for cheap fares).

Buses are the budget fallback. National Express and Megabus run London to Manchester for as low as £5, but it’s a 5-hour slog. I’ve done it twice—once to save money, once because trains were on strike. The seats are cramped, and the WiFi rarely works. If you’re under 25, a Young Persons Railcard (£30 for a year) cuts train fares by a third and pays for itself after two long journeys.

  • London to Edinburgh: LNER train from King’s Cross, advance tickets from £40
  • London to Manchester: Avanti West Coast from Euston, advance from £25
  • Budget option: Megabus or National Express, from £5 but 5+ hours
  • Railcard tip: 16-25 or Two Together card saves 1/3 on off-peak fares

How do I get around London without wasting money?

Don’t buy single tickets. Use a contactless card or phone—the system caps your daily spending automatically. I tapped my phone on the Tube from Paddington to Camden Town last week and paid £3.30 for the whole day’s travel (the cap kicks in after a few rides). The Oyster card works too, but contactless is identical and one less thing to carry.

The Tube is fastest for long distances, but buses are better for seeing the city. The Route 9 bus from Kensington to Trafalgar Square goes past the Royal Albert Hall and Hyde Park—costs £1.75 and you get a free sightseeing tour. Avoid the Elizabeth Line for short hops; it’s great for Heathrow but the stations are deep and the escalators are long.

  • Best for short trips: Contactless card (daily cap ~£8.50 zones 1-2)
  • Scenic bus: Route 9 from Kensington to Trafalgar Square
  • Avoid: Buying paper single tickets at machines—overpriced and slow
  • Tube hack: Stand on the right on escalators, walk on the left

Is it worth renting a car in the UK?

Only if you’re leaving the cities. I rented from Enterprise in Manchester for a trip to the Peak District—£40 a day for a small automatic, and it was the best decision of that week. Driving in central Manchester or London is a nightmare: congestion charges, bus lanes, and parking that costs more than the rental. In Edinburgh, the Old Town is pedestrianized in parts, and the Royal Mile is a crawl of tour buses.

For a road trip through the Lake District or Scottish Highlands, a car is essential. Trains don’t run to most villages, and buses are infrequent. I drove from Edinburgh to Fort William last summer—took three hours, and the scenery near Glencoe was worth every penny of the rental. Just remember: you drive on the left, and roundabouts are everywhere.

  • Best for: Highlands, Lake District, Peak District
  • Avoid in: Central London, Manchester city centre, Edinburgh Old Town
  • Rental tip: Book automatic cars early—they sell out, and manual is common here
  • Cost: £30-50/day plus fuel (£1.50/litre); add £12.50 for London Congestion Charge

What’s the easiest way to get from London to Edinburgh?

The train. I’ve done the flight once—it’s faster in the air (1 hour 20 minutes), but you lose time getting to and from airports. Stansted to Edinburgh Airport is a bus ride away from the city, and security queues are unpredictable. The LNER train from King’s Cross to Edinburgh Waverley drops you right on the Royal Mile. I booked a 7 AM departure last month, had a coffee in the station’s Plaza Kiosco, and was in Edinburgh by 11:30 AM.

If you’re on a tight budget, the Caledonian Sleeper is an option—I took it once for the novelty. It leaves London Euston at 11 PM and arrives in Edinburgh at 7 AM. The cabin was tiny but quiet, and I saved a night’s hotel cost. It’s not cheap (£100+ for a berth), but it beats a hostel.

  • Fastest: LNER train, 4.5 hours, £40-100 advance
  • Cheapest: Megabus, 8+ hours, from £10
  • Unique: Caledonian Sleeper, overnight, £100+ for a cabin
  • Pro tip: Book LNER advance tickets 12 weeks ahead for best prices

How do I use buses in Manchester?

Manchester’s bus system is run by multiple operators, and it’s confusing at first. Stagecoach and First Bus cover most routes, but your ticket from one won’t work on the other. I use the Bee Network app—it’s the local transport authority’s portal and shows real-time arrivals. A single ride is £2, capped at £5 for a day pass within the city.

The V1 bus from Piccadilly Gardens to the Trafford Centre is handy for shopping, and the 43 bus goes from the city centre to Manchester Airport in 30 minutes for £2.50. Avoid the Magic Bus—it’s cheap (£1) but unreliable. I waited 40 minutes for one that never showed, then walked to a Stagecoach stop.

  • Best app: Bee Network for routes and tickets
  • Key route: Bus 43 to Manchester Airport
  • Avoid: Magic Bus (unreliable)
  • Cost: £2 single, £5 day pass

FAQ

Can I use my contactless card from another country on the Tube? Yes, if it has the contactless symbol. I’ve used a US Visa card and a Canadian Mastercard on London’s Tube and buses. It works the same as a UK card—just tap in and out. The daily cap applies, but your bank might charge a foreign transaction fee (usually 1-3%). Check before you travel.

Do I need to book train tickets in advance? For long-distance trains like London to Edinburgh or Manchester, yes—walk-up fares are 2-3 times more expensive. I bought a last-minute ticket from Manchester to London at the station and paid £120. Same trip booked two weeks ahead was £35. For local trains within a city (e.g., Manchester to Stockport), buying on the day is fine.

Is driving in Edinburgh’s Old Town a bad idea? Yes. The streets are narrow, cobblestoned, and full of pedestrians. I drove down the Royal Mile once to reach a hotel near Edinburgh Castle and spent 20 minutes stuck behind a tour bus. Parking is scarce and expensive (£4-5 per hour). Park on the outskirts and take the bus or walk—the city is very walkable.
