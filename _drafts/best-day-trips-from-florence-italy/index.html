Best Day Trips from Florence for First-Time Visitors

I spent three weeks based in Florence, using the Santa Maria Novella station as my launchpad for day trips. Each outing taught me something: some towns need a full day, others just three hours. Here’s what I learned about the five most popular day trips from Florence — what’s worth your time, what’s overhyped, and exactly how to pull each one off without wasting daylight.

How do I get from Florence to Siena?

The bus from Florence’s bus station (right next to Santa Maria Novella) is faster than the train. I took the Tiemme Siena bus from the Autostazione di Firenze — it drops you at Piazza Gramsci in Siena in about 75 minutes. The train goes via Empoli and takes closer to 90-100 minutes, plus the Siena train station is a steep 15-minute uphill walk from the old town. Buy your bus ticket at the tabacchi inside the station, not online — the machine is finicky.

Once in Siena, head straight for Piazza del Campo, the shell-shaped main square. I climbed the Torre del Mangia (€10, 400 steps) at 10 a.m. to beat the heat and crowd. After that, walk to Duomo di Siena — the Piccolomini Library inside has frescoes that rival the Vatican. For lunch, skip the tourist spots on the square and walk to Osteria Le Logge on Via del Porrione. Their pici cacio e pepe was the best pasta I had in Tuscany.

  • Bus from Florence: 75 minutes, €7.80 one way
  • Torre del Mangia: 400 steps, open 10 a.m.–7 p.m.
  • Duomo di Siena: €8 entry, Piccolomini Library included
  • Osteria Le Logge: Via del Porrione 33, book ahead

Is San Gimignano worth a day trip from Florence?

Yes, but only if you’re okay with crowds by midday. San Gimignano is small — you can walk the entire walled town in under an hour — but the skyline of medieval towers is genuinely unique. I went on a Tuesday in early June, and by 11 a.m. the main street was shoulder-to-shoulder. Go early.

Take the Trenitalia regional train from Florence to Poggibonsi (about 40 minutes), then catch the Tiemme 130 bus up the hill to San Gimignano (20 minutes). The bus stop is right outside Poggibonsi station. I bought a San Gimignano Pass (€13) at the tourist office near Piazza della Cisterna — it covers the Torre Grossa climb and the Collegiata church. The tower view is worth it: you can see Siena on a clear day.

For gelato, everyone lines up at Gelateria Dondoli on Piazza della Cisterna. It won the world championship a few times. I tried the crema di Santa Fina (saffron and pine nuts) — it’s good, but not worth a 20-minute queue. Skip it if the line is long and try Gelateria Pluripremiata on Via San Giovanni instead.

  • Train + bus combo: 60 minutes total, €10–12 each way
  • San Gimignano Pass: €13, includes Torre Grossa and Collegiata
  • Gelateria Dondoli: Piazza della Cisterna 4, opens at 10 a.m.
  • Gelateria Pluripremiata: Via San Giovanni 27, shorter lines

Should I see the Leaning Tower of Pisa?

The Leaning Tower is exactly what you think it is — a tourist circus. But it’s also genuinely impressive up close, and the Piazza dei Miracoli (the Field of Miracles) is more than just the tower. The Battistero and Duomo are world-class. I spent two hours on the square and that was enough.

From Florence, the Trenitalia Regionale Veloce to Pisa Centrale takes about an hour. Don’t take the Frecciarossa — it stops at Pisa San Rossore, which is closer to the tower, but the high-speed tickets cost four times more. Walk from Pisa Centrale to the tower (20 minutes through the Borgo Stretto area, which has decent shops and cafes). Climbing the tower costs €18 and requires a timed slot — book at least two days ahead in high season.

I’ll be honest: the tower is surrounded by people doing the “holding it up” photo. It’s silly but harmless. If you want a better photo without the crowds, go at 8 a.m. when the gates open. After the square, I walked to Piazza dei Cavalieri — a quieter, more beautiful square where no one takes photos. Then I grabbed a sandwich at L’Ostellino (Via dei Mille 12) and took the next train back.

  • Train from Florence: 1 hour, €8.60 each way (Regionale)
  • Leaning Tower climb: €18, book at opapisa.it
  • Battistero + Duomo combo: €7, includes both
  • L’Ostellino: Via dei Mille 12, open 11 a.m.–3 p.m.

What’s the best way to visit Lucca from Florence?

Lucca is my favorite day trip from Florence. It’s flat, walkable, and the Renaissance city walls are still intact — you can walk or bike the entire 4.2-kilometer loop. I rented a bike at Piazza Santa Maria for €5 an hour and circled the walls twice. The view changes every few hundred meters: gardens, bastions, and glimpses of the Apuan Alps.

Take the Trenitalia Regionale from Florence to Lucca — it’s 80 minutes direct, €7.30 each way. Exit the station, walk straight through Piazza Napoleone, and you’re at the wall in five minutes. Inside the old town, don’t miss the Torre Guinigi — a tower with oak trees growing on top. The climb is 230 steps, and the top is shaded by those trees. It costs €5 and is less crowded than the Leaning Tower.

For lunch, I walked to Via Fillungo, the main shopping street, and turned into Piazza dell’Anfiteatro — an oval square built on Roman ruins. I ate at Osteria Via San Giorgio (Via San Giorgio 15), a tiny place that serves tordelli lucchesi (giant ravioli in meat sauce). Book a table if you’re going after 1 p.m.

  • Train from Florence: 80 minutes, €7.30 each way
  • Bike rental: €5/hour or €15/half-day at Piazza Santa Maria
  • Torre Guinigi: €5, open 9:30 a.m.–6:30 p.m.
  • Osteria Via San Giorgio: Via San Giorgio 15, reservations recommended

Can I combine Siena and San Gimignano in one day?

You can, but I wouldn’t. I tried it and spent more time on buses than in either town. The bus from Siena to San Gimignano takes 50 minutes (Tiemme line 130), but the schedule is sparse — one bus every two hours on weekdays. If you miss it, you’re stuck for a while.

If you really want to combine them, do Siena first (arrive by 9 a.m., leave by 1 p.m.), then bus to San Gimignano for the afternoon. You’ll skip the Duomo in Siena and the tower climb in San Gimignano. That’s the trade-off. I’d rather do one town well — Siena deserves a full day, and San Gimignano is fine in a half-day if you skip the crowds.

  • Siena to San Gimignano bus: 50 minutes, Tiemme line 130
  • Frequency: Every 1–2 hours, check orari.tiemmespa.it
  • My advice: Pick one, spend the extra time walking the backstreets

FAQ

Is it better to book train tickets in advance for day trips from Florence? For regional trains (the ones you’ll take to Pisa, Lucca, and Siena via bus), booking in advance doesn’t save money — tickets are a fixed price and valid for four hours after purchase. Just buy at the station kiosk or use the Trenitalia app five minutes before departure. For high-speed trains like the Frecciarossa to Venice or Rome, advance booking can cut the price in half.

What’s the best day trip from Florence for avoiding crowds? Lucca, by a wide margin. The city walls absorb the crowds, and the bike loop gives you space. Siena’s Piazza del Campo is busy by 11 a.m., and San Gimignano’s main street is a crush by noon. Pisa’s tower square is always packed. Lucca’s backstreets — like Via del Battistero and Piazza dei Mercanti — stay quiet even in summer.

How early should I start a day trip from Florence? Aim to be on the train or bus by 7:30 a.m. That gets you to any of these towns by 9 a.m., which means two hours of sightseeing before the tour buses arrive from Rome and Venice. I caught the 7:10 a.m. Regionale to Lucca and had the Torre Guinigi to myself until 9:30 a.m.

Conclusion

  • Siena is a full-day town — take the bus, climb the tower, eat pici at Osteria Le Logge
  • San Gimignano works as a half-day if you arrive before 9 a.m. — skip the famous gelato queue
  • Pisa is a two-hour stop — climb the tower if you booked ahead, otherwise just see the square
  • Lucca is the best all-around day trip — bike the walls, climb the tree tower, eat tordelli
  • Don’t combine Siena and San Gimignano in one day — the bus schedule will eat your time
