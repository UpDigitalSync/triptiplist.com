10 Days in Thailand: Bangkok, Chiang Mai & Phuket

I spent ten days bouncing between Bangkok’s chaos, Chiang Mai’s calm, and Phuket’s beaches. That’s tight, but doable if you move smart. This itinerary skips the filler and tells you exactly where I stayed, ate, and what I’d do differently.

Is 10 days enough for Bangkok, Chiang Mai, and Phuket?

Yes, but you need to be ruthless with time. I flew between cities—no overnight trains or buses. Bangkok to Chiang Mai is a 1-hour flight, and Chiang Mai to Phuket is about 1.5 hours. I booked morning flights to maximize daylight at each stop.

  • Bangkok (Days 1–3): Land, eat, temples, and street food.
  • Chiang Mai (Days 4–6): Old City walking, a day trip to Doi Suthep, and night bazaar.
  • Phuket (Days 7–10): Beach time, a longtail boat to Phi Phi, and sunset drinks.

I used AirAsia for all domestic legs. Check-in bags cost extra, so I packed light with a carry-on.

Where should I stay in Bangkok?

I based myself in Sukhumvit near the Nana BTS station. It’s central, connected, and has food everywhere. For a quieter option, I’d pick Khao San Road only if you want backpacker vibes and loud music until 2 a.m.

  • Hotel: I stayed at Hotel Once Bangkok in the Charoen Krung area. Rooftop pool, huge rooms, and a short walk to River City for the Chao Phraya ferry.
  • Neighborhood: Thong Lo for higher-end restaurants and craft cocktail bars. Yaowarat (Chinatown) for the best street food—don’t skip Jay Fai if you’re okay queuing for crab omelette.
  • Tip: Skip tuk-tuks in central Bangkok. Use the BTS Skytrain or Grab app. Tuk-tuk drivers quoted me 300 baht for a 10-minute ride; the BTS cost 44 baht.

What’s the best way to see temples in Bangkok without the crowds?

Go early. I hit Wat Pho at 7:30 a.m. and had the Reclining Buddha mostly to myself. By 9 a.m., tour groups flooded in.

  • Wat Pho: The big reclining Buddha and the massage school—get a 30-minute foot massage for 350 baht.
  • Wat Arun: Cross the river via the Tha Tien ferry (4 baht). Climb the central prang for views over the river. I went at sunset and it was packed; sunrise would be better.
  • Grand Palace: Overrated in my opinion. 500 baht entry, crowds shoulder-to-shoulder, and you can’t see the Emerald Buddha up close. If you’ve seen one gilded temple, you’ve seen them all. I’d skip it and spend that time in Wat Saket (Golden Mount) instead—cheaper, fewer people, and a solid view.

How do I spend 3 days in Chiang Mai?

Chiang Mai is slower and cooler (temperature and vibe). I stayed inside the Old City moat at BED Phrasingh Hotel—simple, clean, free water and fruit all day. Walkable to everything.

  • Day 1: Walk the Old City. Hit Wat Chedi Luang (free, massive ruined chedi) and Wat Phra Singh. Evening at Sunday Walking Street (if you’re there on a Sunday)—it’s a huge market on Ratchadamnoen Road. I ate sai oua (northern sausage) and khao soi from a stall near the Three Kings Monument.
  • Day 2: Morning trip to Doi Suthep. I hired a red songthaew from the Old City for 200 baht round-trip. The 306-step naga staircase is a workout, but the temple has gold and views. Afternoon at Chang Mai Night Bazaar—mostly tourist stuff, but good for cheap souvenirs.
  • Day 3: I booked a half-day cooking class at Mama Noi Thai Cookery School. We made pad thai, green curry, and mango sticky rice from scratch. 1,200 baht, including a market tour. Worth every baht.

Warning: Avoid elephant rides or shows that chain animals. I visited Elephant Nature Park (ethical, rescue-based) for a day. It’s 2,500 baht but includes lunch and transport.

Is Phuket worth it, or should I skip to the islands?

Phuket is a launchpad, not a destination. I landed in Phuket Town and immediately wished I’d gone straight to a beach. Patong is loud and full of jet skis. I stayed at The Shore at Katathani in Kata Noi—quiet, private pool villas, and a beach that’s swimmable most days.

  • Day 7: Arrive, check in, and do nothing. I ate dinner at Suay Restaurant in Cherngtalay—creative Thai food, not cheap (1,500 baht for two), but excellent.
  • Day 8: Longtail boat day trip to Ko Phi Phi. I booked through a local agency on Kata Beach for 1,800 baht per person. Includes snorkeling at Maya Bay (crowded but the water is clear) and lunch on Phi Phi Don. The boat ride is 2 hours each way and bumpy—take motion sickness pills.
  • Day 9: Rent a scooter for 300 baht/day and drive the coastal road from Kata to Nai Harn Beach. Stop at Promthep Cape for sunset. I found a little beach shack called The Boathouse Wine & Grill in Kata—overpriced wine but the view is unbeatable.
  • Day 10: Fly out from Phuket International Airport. Leave 3 hours before your flight; the security line can be slow.

What should I eat in Thailand (and where)?

Street food is the real deal. I ate at least two meals a day from stalls and never got sick. Stick to places with high turnover and locals eating there.

  • Bangkok: Thip Samai for pad thai (the classic, 150 baht). Khao Gaeng Jake Puey for curry rice—a tiny shop near Wat Ratchabophit.
  • Chiang Mai: Khao Soi Khun Yai near the Chang Phueak gate—the best khao soi I had (50 baht). Dash! Restaurant for a sit-down meal with a garden setting.
  • Phuket: Raya Restaurant in Phuket Town for crab curry and stir-fried morning glory. Mee Ton Poe for Hokkien mee.

Drink: Cha yen (Thai iced tea) everywhere. Avoid the neon-colored ones—go for the orange version made from real tea.

FAQ

How much money should I budget for 10 days in Thailand? I spent about $60–80 per day, including accommodation, food, transport, and activities. That’s mid-range—private room, two meals out, one paid activity. Budget travelers can do $30–40, luxury more like $150+.

Do I need a visa for Thailand? Most nationalities get 30 days visa-free on arrival by air. Check your passport. I didn’t need to pre-arrange anything. Just show up with a return ticket and a hotel booking.

Is it safe to eat street food? Yes. I ate at stalls every day and never had an issue. Look for stalls with a queue of locals, and avoid anything that’s been sitting out for hours. I always carried hand sanitizer and drank only bottled water.

Conclusion

  • Fly between cities to save time. AirAsia and Nok Air are reliable.
  • Stay in Sukhumvit (Bangkok), Old City (Chiang Mai), and Kata Noi (Phuket) for the best balance of access and quiet.
  • Skip the Grand Palace. Do Wat Pho early instead.
  • Eat street food daily—Thip Samai, Khao Soi Khun Yai, and Raya Restaurant are worth the trip.
  • Book a day trip to Ko Phi Phi from Phuket, but be ready for a long boat ride.
