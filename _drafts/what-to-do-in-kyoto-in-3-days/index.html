Kyoto in 3 Days: Temples, Bamboo, and Real Food

I landed at Kansai Airport with a backpack and a vague plan: hit the big sights in Kyoto without burning out. Three days felt tight, but I packed in Fushimi Inari at dawn, Arashiyama bamboo before the crowds, and a day trip to Nara that actually worked. Here’s exactly how I did it — including the spots worth your time and the ones I’d skip.

Is three days enough for Kyoto?

Three days is tight but doable if you move early and skip the filler. I covered the main zones — Eastern Kyoto, Arashiyama, Fushimi, Gion, and a half-day in Nara — by starting each day at 7 AM. The train system (JR Nara Line, Sagano Line) is fast and cheap. You won’t see every temple, but you’ll hit the iconic ones without feeling like you’re on a death march.

  • Day 1: Eastern Kyoto (Kiyomizu-dera, Yasaka Shrine, Gion at dusk)
  • Day 2: Arashiyama bamboo grove + Fushimi Inari (go early to both)
  • Day 3: Nara day trip + leftover time in Kyoto Station area

What should I do on Day 1 in Kyoto?

I started at Kiyomizu-dera temple before 8 AM. The wooden stage overlooking the city is genuinely impressive, and the crowds don’t show up until 10. From there, I walked down the Ninenzaka and Sannenzaka slopes — stone paths lined with machiya houses that feel like old Kyoto. Stop at Yokoi Mochiya for their matcha mochi; it’s a local shop that’s been around since 1868.

By noon, I was at Yasaka Shrine in Gion. It’s free, quick, and the lantern-lit main hall gives you that classic Kyoto photo without the ticket price. I grabbed lunch at Men-ya Inoichi, a tiny ramen spot near the shrine — the shoyu broth is rich without being heavy.

Afternoon was for Gion itself. I walked Hanami-koji Street and saw a geiko (Kyoto’s term for geisha) duck into a teahouse around 5 PM. No tour needed — just be respectful and don’t block their path. For dinner, I hit Omen Kodai-ji for udon. It’s a chain, but the Kyoto location in a converted townhouse makes it feel special.

How do I tackle Arashiyama and Fushimi Inari in one day?

This is the day that makes or breaks your trip. I did Arashiyama bamboo grove at 6:30 AM. The trains from Kyoto Station start early — take the JR Sagano Line to Saga-Arashiyama Station. The bamboo was nearly empty, and I had the main path to myself for a solid 20 minutes. After that, I walked to Tenryu-ji temple (the garden is better than the bamboo itself, honestly) and then crossed the Togetsukyo Bridge for a coffee at % Arabica — good brew, long line by 9 AM.

By 10 AM, I was back on the train to Fushimi Inari Taisha. This is the big one: thousands of vermillion torii gates winding up a mountain. I hiked to the Yotsutsuji intersection (about 30 minutes up) for the city view, then turned back. The lower sections get packed by noon, but the crowds thin out the higher you go. I ate a skewer of abura-age (fried tofu sushi) from a stall near the entrance — cheap, filling, and better than any sit-down meal in the area.

  • Take the JR Nara Line from Kyoto Station to Inari Station (5 minutes, 150 yen)
  • Bring water — the hike is steep and the vending machines on the mountain charge double
  • Skip the main path if you want solitude; side trails lead to smaller shrines with zero people

Is Gion worth visiting at night?

Yes, but not for the clichéd “geisha chase.” Gion at night is quieter, more atmospheric, and the lantern-lit streets feel like a movie set. I walked Shirakawa Canal after dinner — the willow trees and stone bridges are beautiful without the daytime crowds. Pontocho Alley is the place for food: narrow, neon-lit, and packed with tiny yakitori joints and sake bars.

  • Gion Hatanaka serves a multi-course kaiseki dinner in a machiya — book ahead
  • Bar K-ya is a whisky bar with 300+ bottles, run by a former geisha’s son
  • Don’t take photos of geiko or maiko (apprentice geisha) without permission — it’s rude and they’ll cover their faces

Should I do a day trip to Nara?

I almost skipped Nara, thinking it was a tourist trap with aggressive deer. I’m glad I didn’t. It’s a 45-minute train from Kyoto Station (JR Nara Line, 760 yen one way), and the park is walkable from the station. The deer are pushy — they bow for crackers, which is cute until one nibbles your map — but Todai-ji temple is the real draw. The Great Buddha statue inside is 15 meters tall and genuinely awe-inspiring.

Nakatanidou in Nara’s old town does mochi-pounding demonstrations every hour. They slap the rice cake with wooden mallets while chanting — it’s touristy but fun, and the fresh mochi is soft and warm. I also walked up to Kasuga Taisha shrine, which has hundreds of bronze lanterns covered in moss. Less crowded than the park, more peaceful.

  • Get the deer crackers (shika senbei) from old women near the entrance — 200 yen, cash only
  • Don’t feed the deer anything else; they’ll eat your bag
  • Return to Kyoto by 5 PM to avoid rush hour on the train

Where should I eat in Kyoto for real local food?

Skip the overpriced kaiseki tourist traps and go for the stuff locals actually eat. Nishiki Market is a covered food street with 100+ stalls — I spent a morning grazing on pickles, grilled eel, and matcha soft serve. Ichiran is a chain, but the Kyoto Station location is open 24 hours and perfect for a solo bowl after a long day.

  • Gyoza Hohei in Gion: crispy gyoza with a thin skin, seats 10 people, expect a 30-minute wait
  • Katsukura near Kyoto Station: tonkatsu (pork cutlet) with bottomless cabbage and rice — the set meal is 1,500 yen
  • Saryo Suisen for matcha parfait — layered with jelly, ice cream, and red bean paste, near Kiyomizu-dera

FAQ

Is it better to stay in Kyoto or Osaka for a 3-day trip? Stay in Kyoto. Osaka is a 30-minute train away, but you lose an hour each day commuting. I booked a room at Hotel Gran Ms Kyoto near Kyoto Station — basic, clean, and a 2-minute walk to the JR lines. For a splurge, The Celestine Kyoto Gion puts you right in the geisha district.

Do I need to book tours in advance for Kyoto? For most temples, no — just show up and buy a ticket. The only exception is Kiyomizu-dera during cherry blossom season (late March to early April) when lines hit 45 minutes. I booked a guided walk of Gion through GetYourGuide for the cultural context, but you can do it solo with a map.

What’s the best way to get from Kansai Airport to Kyoto? Take the Haruka limited express train — it’s 75 minutes to Kyoto Station, costs 2,870 yen with the ICOCA card discount, and has luggage racks. Avoid the bus; it’s cheaper but takes 90 minutes and hits traffic. I used an Airalo eSIM for data on the train — worked instantly.

Conclusion

  • Start each day before 8 AM to beat the crowds at Fushimi Inari and Arashiyama bamboo
  • Eat at Nishiki Market, Men-ya Inoichi, and Gyoza Hohei — skip the overpriced kaiseki
  • Nara is a half-day trip worth taking; feed the deer, see Todai-ji, and eat mochi at Nakatanidou
  • Stay near Kyoto Station for train access; Hotel Gran Ms Kyoto is a solid budget pick
  • Use the JR Nara Line and Sagano Line for most moves — no need for a rail pass in 3 days
