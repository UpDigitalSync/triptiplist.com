Best Day Trips from Barcelona: Montserrat & Girona

I spent a month based in Barcelona’s Eixample district, and the city itself is a feast—but the real magic started when I left it behind. Every day trip I took felt like peeling back another layer of Catalonia. Below are the ones I’d do again, with the exact train lines, ticket tricks, and food stops that made the difference between a good day and a great one.

Why take a day trip from Barcelona at all?

Barcelona is loud, crowded, and electric. After three days of Gaudí crowds and late-night tapas, my legs needed space. The train network from Barcelona Sants station is shockingly good. Within 90 minutes you can be hiking a monastery mountain, walking Roman walls, or eating seafood on a quiet cove. Every trip I took cost less than €25 round-trip on Renfe, and none required a rental car. If you’re here for a week, plan at least two day trips—your feet and your wallet will thank you.

Is Montserrat worth the hype?

Yes, but only if you go early. I caught the 8:36 AM R5 train from Plaça d’Espanya, and by 9:30 I was walking into an almost empty monastery courtyard. By 11 AM, the tour buses started pouring in. The black Madonna (La Moreneta) is the main draw, but the real show is the landscape—those jagged, serrated rock formations feel like something from another planet. I spent two hours hiking the Sant Joan trail above the monastery before the crowds arrived. Bring water and decent shoes; the paths are rocky and steep in sections.

  • Train: R5 from Plaça d’Espanya to Montserrat-Aeri (then cable car up) or Monistrol de Montserrat (then rack railway). Both work.
  • Ticket tip: Buy the “Tot Montserrat” combo at the station—it includes train, cable car, and access to the audiovisual exhibit. Saves about €5.
  • Food: Skip the monastery cafeteria. Walk five minutes to Restaurant Montserrat near the funicular station for decent paella and a terrace view.
  • Don’t miss: The boys’ choir (Escolania) sings at 1 PM in the basilica. It’s free and moving.

Is Girona better than Barcelona for a quiet day?

For me, yes. Girona feels like a medieval Barcelona without the tourist crush. I took the high-speed AVE from Barcelona Sants—37 minutes, no reservation needed on regional trains. The old town is a maze of cobblestone alleys and pastel houses along the Onyar River. I walked the city walls (free, and you can see the whole skyline), then had lunch at Txalaka in the Jewish Quarter for pintxos that cost half of what I’d pay in Barcelona. The cathedral steps are famous from Game of Thrones, but the real gem is the Banys Àrabs—a 12th-century Arab bathhouse that costs €3 and is almost always empty.

  • Train: Renfe Rodalies R11 or R2 Nord from Barcelona Sants to Girona. Regional trains run hourly.
  • Lunch: Casa Marieta for traditional Catalan suquet de peix (fish stew). Reservations recommended on weekends.
  • Walk: The Passeig de la Muralla (city wall ramparts) for views. Entrance is free near the cathedral.
  • Time budget: 6 hours is enough to see the old town and eat a proper meal. 8 if you want to visit the cathedral and baths.

What about Figueres for Dalí fans?

If you love surrealism, this is non-negotiable. Figueres is 40 minutes north of Girona on the same train line, so I combined both in one long day. The Teatre-Museu Dalí is a building designed by Dalí himself—the giant eggs on the roof are his signature. Inside, it’s chaotic, brilliant, and overcrowded by noon. I bought tickets online the night before (€15, skip the line) and walked in at 10 AM. By 11:30, the queue stretched around the block. The museum itself is a work of art: rooms with optical illusions, a giant face installation, and the famous Mae West room. A 90-minute visit is enough unless you’re a hardcore fan.

  • Train: Same R11 line from Barcelona Sants to Figueres-Vilafant. Change at Girona if you’re combining.
  • Ticket: Buy online at the official Dalí Museums site. Avoid third-party resellers—they overcharge.
  • Lunch: La Xicra near the museum for €12 menú del día with local botifarra sausage.
  • Warning: The town itself is quiet outside the museum. Don’t plan a full day here unless you’re also hitting Girona.

Should I go to Sitges or Tarragona for the beach?

Sitges if you want a lively beach town with nightlife. Tarragona if you want Roman history with your sand. I did both. Sitges is 30 minutes south on the R2 Sud train. The beaches are clean, the water is calm, and the promenade is packed with seafood restaurants. I ate grilled sardines at El Trull for €9 and spent the afternoon on Platja de Sant Sebastià. Tarragona, 60 minutes south, has a massive Roman amphitheater right on the Mediterranean—you can stand where gladiators fought. The old town’s Balcó del Mediterrani viewpoint is a perfect sunset spot. For a quick dip, head to Platja del Miracle below the amphitheater.

  • Sitges train: R2 Sud from Passeig de Gràcia or Sants. Every 20 minutes.
  • Tarragona train: R14 or R16 from Sants. About 1 hour, €8 one-way.
  • Food in Tarragona: Barquet for romesco sauce and grilled vegetables. Family-run, no English menu—just point.
  • Best combo: Morning in Tarragona’s Roman ruins, afternoon on the beach, train back by 7 PM.

Can you do a Costa Brava day trip without a car?

Tricky but doable. I took a bus from Barcelona’s Estació del Nord to Tossa de Mar (1.5 hours, €12 each way with Moventis). Tossa’s old town is walled and sits on a rocky peninsula—the Vila Vella is a medieval fortress with turrets and a lighthouse. The coves around the town are stunning: Cala Giverola is a 20-minute walk north with clear water and fewer crowds. I packed a sandwich from Forn de Pa Tossa and ate it on the rocks. The bus back was full by 4 PM, so I’d recommend catching the 3:30 PM departure to avoid standing.

  • Bus: Moventis from Estació del Nord. Book online the day before—weekend seats sell out.
  • Beach: Platja de la Mar Menuda for snorkeling. Rent gear from the shack for €5.
  • Lunch: Can Carlus for grilled calçots (spring onions) in season. Messy, delicious.
  • Skip: The glass-bottom boat tour. Overpriced and the water visibility is hit-or-miss.

FAQ

How do I get to Montserrat from Barcelona without a car? Take the R5 train from Plaça d’Espanya station. At Montserrat-Aeri, switch to the cable car (Aeri de Montserrat) for the final ascent. The whole trip takes about 1 hour 15 minutes. Buy the “Tot Montserrat” ticket at the station for €30—it covers train, cable car, and access to the audiovisual exhibit. Avoid driving; parking is limited and expensive.

Which day trip is best for families with kids? Sitges is the easiest. The train is short (30 minutes), the beaches are gentle (Platja de la Ribera has shallow water), and there’s a playground on the promenade. For older kids, Montserrat’s hiking trails and cable car are a hit. Skip Figueres if your kids are under 10—the Dalí Museum is weird and crowded, and they’ll get bored fast.

Can I combine Girona and Figueres in one day? Yes, and I recommend it. Take the 8 AM R11 train from Barcelona Sants to Girona (37 minutes). Spend 4 hours in Girona (walls, cathedral, Jewish Quarter, lunch). Then catch the 1:30 PM regional train from Girona to Figueres (20 minutes). Visit the Dalí Museum from 2:30 to 4 PM, then take the 4:30 PM train back to Barcelona. Total train time: about 2 hours round trip.

Conclusion

  • Montserrat is best for hikers and early risers—go before 9 AM to beat the crowds.
  • Girona is my top pick for a relaxed, walkable medieval day with great food and no tourist traps.
  • Figueres is a 90-minute surrealist detour for art lovers; combine it with Girona.
  • Sitges and Tarragona offer easy beach access with different vibes—lively vs. historical.
  • Costa Brava is beautiful but requires a bus; plan your return timing carefully to avoid a standing-room ride.
