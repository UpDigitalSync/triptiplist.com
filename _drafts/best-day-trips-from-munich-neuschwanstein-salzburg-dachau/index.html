Best Day Trips from Munich: Neuschwanstein, Salzburg & Dachau

I spent a week based in Munich, and every morning I caught a train out of the city to explore something different. These three day trips—Neuschwanstein Castle, Salzburg, and Dachau—are the most popular, but each requires a different approach. Here’s what worked, what didn’t, and what I’d do differently.

How do you get to Neuschwanstein Castle without wasting half the day?

The castle looks like a Disney postcard, but getting there is a two-hour slog from Munich Hauptbahnhof. I took the Bayernticket (€27 for one person, covers regional trains all day) to Füssen, then hopped on bus 78 to the village of Hohenschwangau. The bus drops you at the ticket center, but don’t queue there—book your castle tour slot online at least a week ahead. I didn’t, and ended up waiting 90 minutes for a 3 PM entry.

  • Train route: Munich Hbf → Füssen (2h, regional train, no reservation needed)
  • Last-mile bus: Bus 78 from Füssen station to Hohenschwangau (10 min, runs every 30 min)
  • Castle access: Online booking mandatory in summer; walk-up tickets sell out by 10 AM
  • Better view: Skip the castle interior and hike to Marienbrücke bridge for the classic photo—free, no queue

The interior tour is underwhelming (14 rooms, 30 minutes, no photos allowed). I’d rather have spent that time walking the Alpsee lake path. Bring a packed lunch—the only restaurant near the ticket office, Schlossbrauhaus, charges €14 for a mediocre schnitzel.

Is Salzburg worth the train ride from Munich?

Yes, but only if you plan ahead. The direct ÖBB Railjet from Munich Hbf to Salzburg Hbf takes 90 minutes and costs €22-€30 one-way if booked a few days early on the ÖBB app. I bought mine same-day and paid €49. Don’t make that mistake.

  • Train: Railjet or Westbahn from Munich Hbf to Salzburg Hbf (1.5h, hourly departures)
  • Old Town walk: Start at Mirabellgarten gardens, cross the Staatsbrücke bridge, then climb to Festung Hohensalzburg fortress (€12 entry or free with Salzburg Card)
  • Lunch: St. Peter Stiftskulinarium (oldest restaurant in Central Europe, €25 for a main) or Bäckerei Essl for a €4 pretzel sandwich
  • Sound of Music tour: Overrated unless you’re a superfan—I skipped it and had a better day

The fortress view is worth the hike, but the city gets packed by noon. I arrived at 8:30 AM and had the Getreidegasse shopping street almost to myself. By 11 AM, it was a shuffle of selfie sticks. Skip the Mozartkugel candies sold on the street—they’re stale. Buy them at Fürst bakery instead.

What should you expect at the Dachau Memorial Site?

This is not a “fun” day trip. I went on a Tuesday morning in October, and the site was somber and quiet. The S-Bahn S2 from Munich Hbf to Dachau station takes 20 minutes, then bus 726 drops you at the memorial gate. Entry is free, but the audioguide (€4) is essential—it explains the layout without overwhelming you with text panels.

  • Transport: S-Bahn S2 to Dachau station (20 min), then bus 726 to “KZ-Gedenkstätte” (10 min)
  • Time needed: 3-4 hours minimum; the site closes at 5 PM (4 PM in winter)
  • Audioguide: €4, available in English, lasts 2.5 hours
  • Key stops: The Jourhaus entrance gate, the reconstructed barracks, the crematorium area, and the museum inside the former maintenance building

I made two mistakes. First, I skipped the 20-minute documentary film at the visitor center—it’s free and provides critical context. Second, I didn’t bring water or snacks. There’s one small café near the exit with limited food. The site is exposed and windy, so dress in layers. Respect the silence—I saw people taking selfies near the crematorium, and it felt wrong.

When is the best time to visit each destination?

Timing makes or breaks these trips. For Neuschwanstein, go in late April or early October—the crowds thin, and the snow hasn’t blocked the Marienbrücke bridge. July and August are a nightmare; I heard from a local that queues hit 2+ hours even with a reservation.

  • Neuschwanstein: April–May or September–October, arrive at Füssen by 9 AM
  • Salzburg: Weekdays in May or September; avoid December unless you want Christmas markets (which are good, but packed)
  • Dachau: Any time except December 24–26 and January 1 (closed); mornings are quieter, and rain actually keeps crowds away

For Salzburg, Sunday is the worst day—most shops in the Old Town close, and the streets fill with day-trippers. I went on a Wednesday and found it manageable.

How do you save money on these day trips?

The Bayernticket is your friend for Neuschwanstein and Dachau (both in Bavaria). It costs €27 for one person or €46 for up to five people. For Salzburg (in Austria), the Bayernticket doesn’t work—you need a separate ticket or the Eurail/Interrail pass if you’re doing multiple countries.

  • Neuschwanstein: Bayernticket (€27 solo, covers regional trains and buses)
  • Dachau: Bayernticket or single S-Bahn ticket (€11 round trip from Munich)
  • Salzburg: Book Railjet early on ÖBB app (€22–€30 one-way) or use FlixBus (€10–€15, but 2.5 hours with traffic)
  • Food: Pack sandwiches from Rischart bakery at Munich Hbf (€5 for a good one) instead of eating at tourist spots

I saved about €40 total by bringing lunch and buying the Bayernticket for two days. The Salzburg train was my biggest expense—book ahead.

FAQ

Is Neuschwanstein Castle overrated? Yes, the interior is a letdown—only 14 furnished rooms, and you can’t take photos. The exterior view from Marienbrücke is the real draw. If you’re short on time, skip the castle tour and just hike the trails around Hohenschwangau. You’ll save €18 and an hour of waiting.

Can you do Salzburg and Dachau in one day? Technically yes, but I wouldn’t. Dachau requires emotional energy, and Salzburg needs a full morning to feel worthwhile. I did Dachau in the morning and spent the afternoon wandering Munich’s English Garden instead. Combining both would mean rushed visits and no time to process.

What’s the best way to get from Munich to Neuschwanstein without a car? Train to Füssen, then bus 78. The Bayernticket covers both. Avoid the guided bus tours from Munich—they cost €60–€80 per person, leave at 8 AM, and herd you through the castle in 45 minutes. I took the train and had an extra hour to explore the village.

Conclusion

  • Neuschwanstein: Book castle tickets online, skip the interior, hike to Marienbrücke, and bring your own food.
  • Salzburg: Take the early Railjet (book ahead), explore the fortress first, and eat at St. Peter Stiftskulinarium or a bakery.
  • Dachau: Allocate 4 hours, use the audioguide, and treat it with the gravity it deserves—no selfies near memorials.
  • Money-saver: Bayernticket for Bavaria trips; book Salzburg train tickets in advance on ÖBB.
  • Timing: Spring and fall for all three; avoid summer weekends at Neuschwanstein and Salzburg.
