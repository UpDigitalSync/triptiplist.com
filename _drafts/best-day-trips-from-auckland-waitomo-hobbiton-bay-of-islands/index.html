Best Day Trips from Auckland: Waitomo, Hobbiton & Bay of Islands

If you’re basing yourself in Auckland and want to see more than the Sky Tower, you’ve got three classic day trips within striking distance. I did all three on separate days and learned exactly what works and what doesn’t. This guide covers Waitomo’s glowworm caves, Hobbiton’s movie set, and the Bay of Islands — with honest advice on driving yourself versus booking a tour, where to eat, and how to dodge the worst crowds.

Is the Waitomo Glowworm Caves tour worth the drive?

Yes, but only if you book the right time slot. The drive from Auckland to Waitomo takes about 2 hours 45 minutes via State Highway 1 and 3. It’s scenic through rolling farmland, but the last 30 minutes are winding back roads — take it slow if you’re prone to car sickness.

The main attraction is the Waitomo Glowworm Caves, a commercial cave system run by a family-owned company. The standard 45-minute tour takes you through limestone chambers then onto a silent boat ride under thousands of glowworms. I found the boat ride genuinely impressive — the glowworms look like a starry sky reflected on water. But the tour moves in big groups (up to 40 people), and the guides rush you through the dry sections.

  • Best time to go: Book the 8:30 AM tour. You’ll be one of the first groups in, which means fewer elbows on the boat.
  • Alternative: Ruakuri Cave (20 minutes south) offers a longer, more adventurous 2-hour tour with spiral staircases and louder underground waterfalls. I preferred this for smaller groups.
  • Skip: The Waitomo Homestead cafe next to the main caves. Overpriced sandwiches. Drive 5 minutes to Huhu Cafe in town for decent coffee and a bacon-butty.
  • Tour vs. self-drive: I drove myself and saved about $80 per person compared to packaged bus tours. Parking is free at the caves.

How do you visit Hobbiton without feeling like a tourist trap?

Hobbiton is a tourist trap — but it’s a really well-run one. Located near Matamata, about 2 hours 15 minutes south of Auckland, the movie set is a purpose-built village from The Lord of the Rings and The Hobbit trilogies. You can only visit on a guided tour, and it’s worth doing exactly once.

The two-hour guided walk takes you through 44 hobbit holes, the Green Dragon Inn, and the Party Tree. Our guide was a local farmer’s daughter who knew every blade of grass — she pointed out details like the fake oak tree built from scratch and the tiny washing lines that made the set feel lived-in. The highlight is the free drink at the Green Dragon Inn: their apple cider is legitimately good.

  • Book the last tour of the day (usually 3:30 PM in summer). You’ll have the path nearly to yourself as the morning crowds thin out.
  • Lunch stop: Matamata’s main street has Cafe Fresca for solid flat whites and a steak sandwich. Avoid the bus-depot-style lunch at the Hobbiton ticket office.
  • Combo tour: Many operators bundle Hobbiton with Waitomo in one long day. I tried this and regretted it — you spend 5 hours on a bus and only get 45 minutes at each site. Do them separately.
  • Ticket price: $89 NZD per adult (2024). Book online at least a week ahead in peak season (December–February).

Is the Bay of Islands doable as a day trip from Auckland?

Technically yes, but it’s a long day — 3.5 hours each way by car to Paihia, the main town. I’d only recommend it if you’re comfortable driving 7+ hours total and leaving Auckland by 6 AM. The reward is a stunning coastal region with clear water, historic towns, and dolphin cruises.

The drive goes north on State Highway 1 through Whangarei. Stop at Kawakawa to see the Hundertwasser Toilets — a quirky public restroom designed by the artist Friedensreich Hundertwasser. It’s free and bizarrely beautiful. Then push on to Paihia, which is the launch point for most water activities.

  • Best single activity: The Hole in the Rock cruise with Explore Group. It’s a 4-hour boat trip from Paihia past the Cape Brett Lighthouse and through a natural rock arch. We saw bottlenose dolphins on both my visits.
  • Historic stop: Waitangi Treaty Grounds (5 minutes from Paihia). The museum is excellent for understanding New Zealand’s founding document, but the guided tour of the Treaty House felt rushed. Skip it and just walk the grounds.
  • Lunch in Paihia: The Gables on the waterfront does fresh fish and chips with a view of the bay. Avoid the tourist-trap ice cream shops on the main strip.
  • Return timing: Leave Paihia by 4 PM to avoid driving the winding coastal roads in the dark. You’ll be back in Auckland by 7:30 PM.

What’s the best way to combine these trips if you have limited time?

If you only have two days, skip the Bay of Islands and focus on Waitomo and Hobbiton. They’re close enough to each other (about 1 hour 15 minutes apart) that you can do one per day without burnout. Here’s a realistic two-day plan:

  • Day 1: Drive Auckland to Waitomo (2:45 AM departure). Do the 8:30 AM Ruakuri Cave tour. Lunch at Huhu Cafe. Drive to Hamilton (1 hour) and spend the night. I stayed at Ibis Hamilton Tainui — basic but clean, with a casino downstairs if that’s your thing.
  • Day 2: Drive Hamilton to Matamata (45 minutes). Book the 9:30 AM Hobbiton tour. Lunch at Cafe Fresca. Drive back to Auckland (2 hours 15 minutes) by 3 PM.

For the Bay of Islands, you need a full day — no shortcuts. If you have three days, do Waitomo + Hobbiton on days 1 and 2, then Bay of Islands on day 3. That’s a packed but manageable itinerary.

FAQ

Should I rent a car or join a tour for these day trips? I rented a car from Apex Car Rentals at Auckland Airport for about $60 NZD per day. It gave me flexibility to start early and stop at random cafes. Group tours (like GreatSights or Gray Line) cost $150–$200 per person and include pickup from central Auckland hotels, but you’re locked into their schedule. If you hate driving, take the tour. If you want to eat when you want, drive yourself.

What time should I leave Auckland to avoid traffic? Leave by 6:30 AM for any direction. Auckland’s motorway (State Highway 1) jams up from 7:00–9:00 AM, especially around the Harbour Bridge and Spaghetti Junction (the interchange near Greenlane). For Waitomo and Hobbiton, you’re heading south, which is slightly better than northbound traffic, but still leave early.

Can I visit Hobbiton without a booking? No. Walk-ups are rarely accepted, especially in summer. Book online at hobbitontours.com at least two weeks ahead for December–February. For off-season (May–August), you can usually book a day or two before.
