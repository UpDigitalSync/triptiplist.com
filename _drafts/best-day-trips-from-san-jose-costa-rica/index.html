Best Day Trips from San José: Volcanoes, Waterfalls & Island

If you’re based in San José and itching to see more of Costa Rica without packing a suitcase, you’ve got options. I spent a week using the capital as a hub, and three day trips stood out: a smoldering volcano, a misty waterfall complex, and a Caribbean-style island escape. Each one works on a different kind of day — here’s what I learned, what I’d do again, and what I’d skip.

Is Poás Volcano worth the early wake-up?

Yes, but only if you go on a clear morning. Poás Volcano National Park sits about 90 minutes northwest of San José, and the main attraction is the 1.5-kilometer-wide crater with a turquoise acid lake. I left the city at 6 a.m. and was glad I did — by 9 a.m., the parking lot was full and the clouds had rolled in.

The park is small. You walk a paved path to the main crater viewpoint (maybe 10 minutes), then a separate short trail to Lake Botos, an extinct crater lake surrounded by cloud forest. The whole visit takes about two hours.

  • Good to know: The park limits daily visitors to 1,000. Book your entry ticket online at the SINAC website ahead of time — they sell out days in advance.
  • What to bring: A rain jacket, even if the forecast says sun. The wind at 2,700 meters is cold, and drizzle comes fast.
  • Where to eat: Skip the overpriced park cafeteria. Drive 10 minutes down the road to Restaurante Chubascos for a casado (rice, beans, plantain, protein) that costs half the price and tastes twice as good.
  • Honest take: The crater is impressive, but the experience feels a bit like a theme-park attraction — orderly, short, and crowded. If you’ve seen other volcanoes (Arenal, Irazú), Poás might feel underwhelming.

What makes La Paz Waterfall Gardens different from other waterfall parks?

La Paz Waterfall Gardens is a private nature reserve about an hour north of San José, and it’s the most polished day-trip attraction I visited in Costa Rica. It’s not a single waterfall — it’s a network of paved trails that connect five major falls, plus a butterfly observatory, a hummingbird garden, and a rescue center for jungle cats.

I walked the main loop in about 2.5 hours, stopping at every waterfall platform. The path is steep in sections but well-maintained, with handrails and stairs. The Magia Blanca waterfall is the tallest at 37 meters, and the spray will soak you if you get close.

  • The best part: The hummingbird garden. Dozens of species feed from hanging feeders a foot from your face. I stood there for 20 minutes.
  • The catch: Entry is expensive — $48 per adult as of my visit. It’s a private operation, not a national park.
  • Where to eat: The on-site Restaurant La Paz serves a solid buffet lunch (rice, beans, grilled chicken, fresh fruit) with a view of the main waterfall. It’s $18 per person, and it’s worth it if you’re hungry.
  • Honest take: La Paz is a well-oiled machine. It feels curated, not wild. If you want raw nature, skip it. If you want a safe, photogenic, family-friendly day with guaranteed wildlife sightings, it’s perfect.

Can you really do Tortuga Island as a day trip from San José?

Yes, but prepare for a long day. Tortuga Island lies off the Nicoya Peninsula, about 3.5 hours from San José by bus and boat combined. Most tour operators pick you up from your hotel in San José around 6 a.m., drive to Puntarenas, then ferry you across the Gulf of Nicoya to the island.

The island itself is small — white sand, turquoise water, and a handful of thatched-roof restaurants. You’ll get about 3 to 4 hours on the beach, plus a snorkeling stop on the way back. The water is calm and clear, and I saw schools of angelfish and a sea turtle within ten minutes of snorkeling.

  • Tour operators: I went with Tortuga Island Day Tour (booked through my hotel). They handled transport, lunch, and snorkel gear. Cost was $95 per person.
  • What’s included: Round-trip transport from San José, a boat ride with drinks, a buffet lunch (rice, fish, salad, fruit), and snorkeling equipment.
  • What’s not included: Tips, extra drinks, and the optional banana-boat ride ($15 extra).
  • Honest take: The beach is beautiful but not pristine — expect crowds, music from other boats, and vendors selling trinkets. It’s a party vibe, not a secluded paradise. If you want quiet, skip Tortuga and go to Manuel Antonio instead.

When is the best time for these day trips?

Dry season (December to April) is best for all three. Poás is often socked in by clouds during the rainy season (May to November), and you might see nothing but gray. La Paz is less affected — waterfalls actually run fuller in the wet season — but trails get slippery. Tortuga Island is a gamble in rainy months; afternoon thunderstorms can cancel the boat trip.

For Poás, aim for a weekday morning. Weekends draw huge crowds. For Tortuga, check the marine forecast — if winds are high, the crossing gets choppy. I took Dramamine and still felt queasy on the return trip.

What should I pack for a day trip from San José?

The essentials vary by destination, but a few items worked for all three:

  • Rain jacket (cheap poncho works too)
  • Closed-toe shoes with grip (trails at La Paz and Poás are wet)
  • Reusable water bottle (most parks have refill stations)
  • Cash (small bills for snacks, tips, and park entry fees)
  • Sunscreen and insect repellent (especially for Tortuga)
  • Dry bag (for your phone and wallet on the boat to Tortuga)

FAQ

How do I get to Poás Volcano without a car? Book a shared shuttle or a group tour from San José. Most hotels can arrange pick-up. The drive takes about 90 minutes, and shuttles cost around $45 per person round-trip. Driving yourself is doable — the road is paved all the way — but the park entrance is easy to miss if you’re not watching for the sign.

Is La Paz Waterfall Gardens suitable for kids or elderly visitors? Yes, with caveats. The main trail has stairs and steep sections, but there’s a shorter accessible path that reaches the first waterfall. The butterfly garden and hummingbird area are flat and easy. I saw families with strollers and older couples with walking sticks — it’s manageable if you go slow.

Can I combine Poás Volcano and La Paz in one day? I tried this, and I don’t recommend it. They’re only about 30 minutes apart by car, but the timing is tight. Poás requires a morning visit (before clouds roll in), and La Paz needs at least three hours. You’ll be rushed and tired. Pick one and enjoy it.

Conclusion

  • Poás Volcano is a quick, scenic stop best done on a clear weekday morning — book your entry online in advance.
  • La Paz Waterfall Gardens offers the most polished experience with guaranteed wildlife and easy trails, but it’s pricey and curated.
  • Tortuga Island delivers a beach day with snorkeling, but the long travel time and crowds make it a trade-off — not a must-do.
  • Pack a rain jacket and cash for all three; each trip requires an early start.
  • If you have only one day, choose La Paz. It’s the most bang for your time and effort.
