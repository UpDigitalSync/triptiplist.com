Where to Stay in Bergen: Best Neighborhoods for Every Budget

I’ve been to Bergen three times now, and each trip taught me something new about where to lay your head. The first time I booked a hotel near the fish market thinking “central” meant “best.” It wasn’t. The second time I stayed up the hill in Fjellsiden and finally understood why locals smile when they talk about their morning commute. This guide is built from those mistakes and discoveries — no fluff, just the real trade-offs between budget, convenience, and atmosphere in Norway’s rainiest city.

What’s the difference between staying in the city center (Sentrum) and the old town (Bryggen)?

Sentrum is Bergen’s commercial core — think chain stores, the main train station, and the bus terminal. It’s convenient but feels like any small European city center. Bryggen, technically part of Sentrum, is the UNESCO-listed wooden wharf. They’re a five-minute walk apart, but the vibe shifts completely.

  • Sentrum pros: Direct access to Fløibanen funicular, the Light Rail to the airport, and most bus lines. You’ll never walk more than ten minutes to a restaurant.
  • Sentrum cons: Can feel dead after 8 PM outside the main square. The area around the bus station has a few rough edges at night.
  • Bryggen pros: You wake up to postcard views. The old wooden buildings glow at sunset. Restaurants like Bryggen Tracteursted serve solid traditional food in a 300-year-old cellar.
  • Bryggen cons: Tourist crowds from 10 AM to 6 PM. Accommodation here is expensive and often in creaky old buildings with thin walls. I heard my neighbor snoring at Det Hanseatiske Hotel.

For first-timers, I’d pick a hotel on the edge of Sentrum near the Torgallmenningen square — close enough to walk to Bryggen in five minutes but quiet enough to sleep. Hotel Norge or Clarion Collection Havnekontoret both fit that sweet spot.

Is staying near the Fish Market worth the premium?

The Fisketorget (Fish Market) is Bergen’s most famous food destination, but I’d think twice before booking a room right on top of it. During summer, cruise ship crowds turn the area into a scrum of selfie sticks and overpriced shrimp sandwiches. A plate of fish soup can run 250 NOK ($23), and it’s rarely the best in town.

  • Zander K hotel sits right on the market square. Great location for sightseeing, but you’ll pay for the address — rooms start around 2000 NOK per night.
  • The Hanseatic Hotel is literally inside a Bryggen building. Charming, but the stairs are steep and there’s no elevator. Not ideal if you have heavy luggage.
  • Better value: walk five minutes to Nedre Korskirkeallmenningen for Bare Vestland, a local spot with better fish soup at 185 NOK.
  • If you must stay nearby, book a room facing the back alley, not the market. The noise from fishmongers starts at 6 AM.

I stayed at Thon Hotel Rosenkrantz one block away, and it was the perfect compromise — their breakfast buffet is legendary (seriously, it’s worth setting an alarm for), and the rooms are modern with good soundproofing.

Where should budget travelers stay in Bergen?

Bergen is expensive. There’s no way around it. But you can cut costs without sleeping in a hostel dorm if you know where to look.

  • Montana Family & Youth Hostel sits on Ulriksveien in the Årstad district, a 15-minute bus ride from the center. It’s cheap (around 350 NOK for a dorm bed), has a kitchen, and the bus stop is right outside. The view from the common room over the city is legit.
  • Citybox Bergen in Sentrum is a no-frills hotel with self-check-in, small rooms, and prices from 900 NOK. It’s clean, central, and you pay for exactly what you use — no breakfast, no lobby, no nonsense.
  • Marken Gjesthus is a guesthouse near the train station. Dorm beds from 300 NOK, private rooms from 800 NOK. The shared bathrooms are clean, and the staff let you store luggage after checkout.
  • For something between budget and mid-range, try Comfort Hotel Bergen on Christies gate. Rooms are compact but well-designed, and the free coffee in the lobby is a lifesaver before heading out in the rain.

My pro tip: book a room without breakfast and grab a cinnamon bun and coffee at Godt Brød on Marken for 60 NOK total. You’ll save 150 NOK compared to hotel breakfasts.

What’s the best neighborhood for families or longer stays?

If you’re staying more than three nights or traveling with kids, the city center gets claustrophobic. Bergen’s residential neighborhoods offer more space, playgrounds, and actual grocery stores where locals shop.

  • Fjellsiden (the hillside above the city) is my top pick. It’s a ten-minute uphill walk from the center, but the views over the harbor are worth every step. Fjellveien is a scenic walking path that runs along the ridge — perfect for strollers or morning jogs.
  • Nordnes is a peninsula jutting into the harbor, a 15-minute walk from Bryggen. It’s quiet, leafy, and has a small beach (Nordnes Sjøbad) where locals swim in summer. Bergen Børs Hotel here is pricey but family-friendly with connecting rooms.
  • Sandviken is north of Bryggen, a bit more bohemian. Sandviken Bryggespiler serves excellent mussels, and the area feels like a village within the city. Airbnb apartments here are cheaper than Sentrum equivalents.
  • Årstad near Fantoft Stave Church is further out but has the Fantoft student village vibe — cheap eats, a Rema 1000 supermarket, and bus connections every ten minutes.

For a family of four, I rented an Airbnb on Klosteret in Nordnes for a week. We cooked dinner most nights using ingredients from the Mathallen food hall, walked to the aquarium in ten minutes, and never felt crowded.

Should I stay near the airport or the cruise port?

The airport question comes up every time. Bergen Airport (Flesland) is 20 minutes by Light Rail from the city center. The train runs every 15 minutes and costs 42 NOK — cheaper than a taxi (which starts at 400 NOK). Unless your flight arrives after midnight (last Light Rail leaves around 11:30 PM), there’s no reason to stay near the airport.

  • The Scandic Bergen City hotel is a five-minute walk from the Light Rail stop at Byparken. Perfect for early flights.
  • If you have a late arrival, Comfort Hotel Bergen Airport is literally connected to the terminal. It’s basic but functional.
  • Cruise passengers should stay near Skoltegrunnskaien or Jekteviksbakken — the main cruise terminals. Radisson Blu Royal is the closest, but Clarion Collection Hotel on Rosenkrantz gate is a shorter walk to the Bryggen sights.

One warning: the cruise port area is dead after the ships leave. If you want evening life, stay in Sentrum or Fjellsiden instead.

FAQ

Is Bergen safe to walk around at night? Yes. Bergen is one of the safest cities I’ve visited. I walked from Bryggen to my hotel in Fjellsiden at midnight without any issues. The main risk is slippery cobblestones when it rains — which is often. Wear shoes with good grip. The area around the bus station can feel a bit sketchy after 10 PM, but I never saw anything dangerous.

How many days should I stay in Bergen? Two full days is enough for the main sights (Fløibanen, Bryggen, the Fish Market). Add a third day if you want to take a fjord cruise to Mostraumen or the Sognefjord. I’d recommend three nights — arrive on day one, explore on day two, take a day trip on day three, and leave on day four. More than that and you’ll run out of things to do unless you’re hiking.

What’s the best way to get from Bergen Airport to the city center? The Bybanen Light Rail is the answer. It leaves from right outside the terminal, runs every 7-15 minutes, and drops you at Byparken in the center in 45 minutes. A single ticket costs 42 NOK (buy it from the machine at the station or use the Skyss app). Taxis cost 400-600 NOK and take 25 minutes. Uber doesn’t operate in Bergen. The airport bus (Flybussen) is slightly faster than the Light Rail but costs 130 NOK — skip it.

Conclusion

  • First-timers should stay near Torgallmenningen square — central enough for walking, quiet enough for sleeping.
  • Budget travelers get the best value at Citybox Bergen or Montana Youth Hostel, with breakfast from Godt Brød.
  • Families should book an Airbnb in Nordnes or Sandviken for space and kitchen access.
  • Avoid staying right on the Fish Market or near the cruise port unless you have a specific reason.
  • Do not stay near the airport — the Light Rail makes it unnecessary.
