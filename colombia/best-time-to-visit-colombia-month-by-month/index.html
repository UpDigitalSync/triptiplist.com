Best Time to Visit Colombia: Month-by-Month Guide for Travelers

I spent three months bouncing between Bogotá’s high-altitude chill, Medellín’s eternal spring, and Cartagena’s tropical steam bath. What I learned: Colombia doesn’t have one “best” time—it has three microclimates that shift month to month. Here’s the no-nonsense breakdown so you can pick your window and pack right.

When is the best overall time to visit Colombia?

December through March is the sweet spot for most travelers. The Caribbean coast (Cartagena) gets its driest stretch, Medellín stays sunny, and Bogotá sees fewer afternoon downpours. You’ll pay more for flights and hotels, but you earn it back with reliable weather.

  • December–March: Peak dry season nationwide. Cartagena is hot and humid but rain-free. Medellín averages 22°C (72°F) with blue skies. Bogotá can still get chilly evenings—pack layers.
  • July–August: Secondary dry window, especially on the coast. Prices dip slightly versus December. Medellín’s Feria de las Flores happens in early August.
  • April–June & October–November: Rainy months. Bogotá gets drizzly, Medellín sees afternoon showers, Cartagena feels sticky. You’ll find empty beaches and half-price hotels in El Poblado.

What is Bogotá like month by month?

Bogotá sits at 2,600 meters, so “rainy” means cold drizzle, not tropical downpours. I stayed in La Candelaria during January—crisp mornings, sunny afternoons, and the Monserrate cable car ran without fog. By April, I was dodging showers daily.

  • January–February: Driest months. Perfect for walking the Usaquén Sunday market and eating ajiaco at La Puerta de la Candelaria.
  • March–May: Rain ramps up. Afternoons are wet. I’d recommend indoor plans—Museo del Oro and Museo Botero are solid. The Ciclovía (Sunday bike closures) still runs.
  • June–August: Cool and partly cloudy. Lower crowds at Plaza de Bolívar. July is actually one of the less rainy months.
  • September–November: Wettest stretch. I skipped Bogotá in October—constant overcast and drizzle.
  • December: Festive but rainy. Zona Rosa bars get packed for Christmas lights. Book Hotel de la Ópera in La Candelaria for proximity to everything.

When should I visit Medellín for the best weather?

Medellín is the “City of Eternal Spring” for a reason. I spent February there and barely saw a cloudy day. The Metrocable ride up to Santo Domingo was crystal clear. That said, “eternal” doesn’t mean dry—rain comes February through May and September through November.

  • December–February: Peak dry season. 24°C (75°F) every day. I stayed in El Poblado at The Charlee Hotel and used the rooftop pool daily.
  • March–May: Short afternoon showers. Still very pleasant. Comuna 13 graffiti tour is fine in light rain—wear shoes with grip for the escalators.
  • June–August: Dry-ish and packed. August’s Feria de las Flores brings parades and chaos. Book Hotel Patio del Mundo in Laureles months ahead.
  • September–November: Rainiest months. I’d avoid November. Parque Arví gets muddy. Stick to Mercado del Río food hall for indoor grazing.

What’s the best time for Cartagena’s beaches and heat?

Cartagena is brutally humid year-round. The difference is rain. I went in January—35°C (95°F) with no clouds. The Walled City was sweat-soaked by 10 a.m., but Playa Blanca was perfect. March through May? I’d skip it unless you love afternoon monsoons.

  • December–April: Dry season. Expect wall-to-wall sun. Getsemaní neighborhood is lively. Eat ceviche at La Cevichería on Calle del Sargento.
  • May–June: Transition months. Rain picks up. Castillo San Felipe de Barajas is less crowded but hot inside.
  • July–August: Secondary dry window. Good for Islas del Rosario day trips. Stay at Casa Pestagua in the Walled City for a pool escape.
  • September–November: Wettest. I saw streets flood in October. Bazurto Market is an adventure in any weather, but bring waterproof shoes.

How do festivals affect the best time to visit?

Colombia’s calendar is packed with events that spike prices and crowds. I accidentally hit Barranquilla Carnival in February—hotels tripled. Medellín’s Feria de las Flores in August is worth it if you book early. Bogotá’s Rock al Parque in July is free and draws massive crowds.

  • January: Feria de Cali (salsa festival) wraps up early. Cartagena’s Hay Festival brings literary crowds.
  • February/March: Carnaval de Barranquilla (floats, costumes, chaos). Skip if you want quiet.
  • August: Feria de las Flores in Medellín. Book Hotel Dann Carlton in El Poblado six months out.
  • December: Feria de Cali starts. Alumbrados Navideños (Christmas lights) in Medellín’s Parque del Río are stunning.

What should I pack for each season?

I learned the hard way: Colombia’s altitude variation means you need a wardrobe for three climates. In Bogotá, I froze in a thin jacket. In Cartagena, I sweated through every shirt. Medellín was perfect with a light hoodie.

  • Bogotá (year-round): Layers. Fleece, waterproof jacket, jeans. Umbrella every single day.
  • Medellín (dry months): T-shirts, shorts, one light sweater for evenings. Rain jacket for March–May.
  • Cartagena (year-round): Linen, shorts, sandals, swim trunks. Hat and sunscreen. Rain jacket if visiting May–November.
  • General tip: Good walking shoes. Cobblestones in Cartagena’s Walled City and Medellín’s hills wrecked my sneakers.

FAQ

Is Colombia safe to visit during the rainy season? Yes. Rain doesn’t make Colombia less safe—it just makes some roads muddy and outdoor plans soggy. I walked around La Candelaria in Bogotá during heavy October rain and felt fine. Stick to well-lit areas and use Uber or official taxis at night. The bigger risk is flash flooding in low-lying Cartagena streets; avoid walking through standing water.

Should I book tours ahead or on arrival? On arrival for flexibility, but ahead for popular spots. I booked the Comuna 13 graffiti tour in Medellín same-day—easy. For Lost City Trek (Ciudad Perdida) or Cartagena’s Rosario Islands, book a week ahead through a reputable operator like GetYourGuide to avoid bait-and-switch. Rainy season tours often run, but check cancellation policies.

Can I visit all three cities in one trip? Yes, but pace yourself. I did Bogotá (3 days), Medellín (4 days), Cartagena (3 days) in ten days. Fly between them—buses take 12+ hours. Bogotá to Medellín is a 50-minute flight; Medellín to Cartagena is 1 hour. Use Avianca or LATAM. Avoid connecting through El Dorado Airport during peak hours (5–8 p.m.)—it’s a zoo.

Conclusion

  • December–March is the safest bet for dry weather across all three cities, but book hotels in El Poblado and Getsemaní early.
  • July–August gives you good weather with fewer crowds, especially in Cartagena and Medellín.
  • Rainy months (April–June, October–November) mean cheap flights and empty sites—just pack a rain jacket and flexible plans.
  • Festivals like Feria de las Flores or Carnaval de Barranquilla are incredible but spike costs—book transport and lodging months ahead.
  • Pack for three climates regardless of season: Bogotá’s cold, Medellín’s mild, Cartagena’s heat. Layers are your friend.
